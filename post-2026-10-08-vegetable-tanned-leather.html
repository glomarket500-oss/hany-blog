《植鞣革：點解我哋揀一種會變色嘅皮》Vegetable-Tanned Leather: Why We Chose a Hide That Changes Colour

2026年10月8日Oct 8, 2026 產品回憶Product Notes 植鞣革Vegetable-Tanned

我做產品二十六年，第一次聽到「揀一塊會變色嘅皮」，反應同大部分人一樣：咁即係有問題？

買嘢嘅常識係：唔應該變。白色要一路白，黑色要一路黑。任何會變色嘅材料，通常都係質素唔穩定、或者會老化。

皮就係例外。而且唔止係例外——「會變」係佢唯一嘅賣點，亦係我最後揀佢嘅唯一理由。

呢篇文我會講清楚：植鞣革係乜、包漿究竟係乜化學反應、六個月同三年之後會變成點，同埋最緊要嘅——佢有幾多缺點，同邊種人唔應該買。如果你睇完覺得唔啱你，我會覺得呢篇文寫得成功。

一、先講清楚「皮」究竟係乜

一張牛皮，剖開係幾層：

  • 表皮同毛——最外面，鞣製前就要去掉，冇用。
  • 粒面層（grain）——最外層真皮，即係毛囊同毛孔所在嘅位置。呢層嘅纖維最細密、最緊，天然紋路就係喺呢度。你聽到嘅「粒面」，就係講緊呢一層。
  • 網狀層（corium／真皮層）——下面嗰層，係粗大嘅膠原纖維束交織成網。呢層提供強度同厚度。一張皮嘅大部分厚度都喺呢度。
  • 肉面層——最底，貼住肉嘅一面，纖維鬆散。

「頭層皮」（full-grain）係指保留咗粒面層嘅嗰一片皮——即係最外層，唔打磨、唔磨走粒面。正因為冇磨過，佢會保留天然嘅痕跡：蚊叮、刮痕、生長紋、頸部同腹部嘅纖維鬆緊差別。呢啲唔係瑕疵，係一張真皮嘅身份證。如果你想要每一塊都一模一樣，你其實係想要塑膠。

把一張皮橫向剖開，就會得到「二層皮」（split）——即係網狀層，冇粒面。呢個係後面講「二層皮」「再生皮」嘅關鍵。

二、植鞣同鉻鞣：兩種化學，兩種性格

生皮本身係會腐爛、乾咗會變硬嘅蛋白質。要變成「皮革」，就要「鞣」——即係用化學方法將膠原蛋白嘅纖維穩定住，令佢唔會腐爛、唔會一遇水就縮成一團。歷史上有兩條主要路線。

植鞣（vegetable tanning）：用植物多酚慢慢滲

植鞣用嘅係植物裡面嘅單寧（tannin），即係多酚類物質。分兩大類：

  • 水解類單寧——例如橡樹五倍子、栗木、塔拉（tara）、訶子（myrobalan）。分子較細，容易滲入皮嘅深層。
  • 縮合類單寧——例如含羞草（mimosa）、白堅木（quebracho）、檳榔膏（gambier）。分子較大，主要負責皮面嘅豐滿同顏色。

佢哋嘅作用機制，唔係「包住」纖維，而係同膠原蛋白嘅醯胺基、肽鍵形成氫鍵，並且把纖維束之間「鎖」起來——纖維之間原本會滑動、遇水會脹，被鎖住之後就穩定咗。呢個過程唔快：傳統做法係一缸一缸咁浸，濃度由低到高，要幾個星期。所以植鞣皮（俗稱「樹膏皮」）出廠就係暖黃、淺棕嗰種色，皮身偏硬、紮實，可以雕刻、壓印、打孔，亦會隨光同時間繼續變深。

鉻鞣（chrome tanning）：用鉻(III)幾個鐘搞完

鉻鞣用嘅係鹼式硫酸鉻(III)。鉻(III) 離子會同膠原蛋白嘅羧基配位結合，形成交聯。呢個反應快得多——幾個鐘就完成，唔使幾個星期。

鉻鞣嘅優點非常實在，我唔會為咗賣植鞣皮而抹黑佢：

  • 耐熱好多。鉻鞣皮嘅收縮溫度係 100 °C 以上嘅級數，植鞣皮係 70 °C 級數。即係話，鉻鞣皮更適合做要浸熱水、要蒸、要定型嘅工序。
  • 耐水、均勻、軟。可以做鮮色、做大量一致嘅產品。
  • 快同便宜。所以全球皮革產量裡面，鉻鞣佔八成以上。你身上大部分皮製品都係鉻鞣。

缺點亦要講清楚：含鉻廢水係真實嘅環境問題，一間鞣革廠嘅污水處理做得好唔好，直接決定呢種皮係唔係「乾淨」。同時要分清兩件事：三價鉻（Cr(III)）同六價鉻（Cr(VI)）完全唔同——Cr(VI) 係致癌物，管制嚴格；Cr(III) 本身毒性低得多，係皮革業正常使用嘅形態。但在不當條件下（例如某些氧化環境、或者廢料焚燒），Cr(III) 有機會轉成 Cr(VI)，所以呢個問題唔可以當冇事，亦唔應該妖魔化到「鉻鞣＝毒皮」。兩邊都係懶惰嘅講法。

兩者對照

植鞣（vegetable-tanned）鉻鞣（chrome-tanned）
鞣劑植物多酚（水解類＋縮合類）鹼式硫酸鉻(III)
時間幾個星期（傳統缸浸）幾個鐘
收縮溫度大約 70 °C 級數100 °C 以上級數
顏色起點暖黃、淺棕，會持續變深可染任何顏色，較穩定
皮身偏硬、紮實、可雕刻軟、均勻、易成型
耐水較差，會留水痕較好
養成（包漿）最典型視乎表面處理，未必
環境製程慢、耗水，但唔涉及鉻含鉻廢水，處理水平差異大

一句總結：鉻鞣係「效率同穩定」嘅答案，植鞣係「性格同時間」嘅答案。手機係一件每日拎幾十次嘅物件，我要嘅係後者。

三、頭層、二層、修正粒面、再生皮：市場上嘅名詞遊戲

呢一節你如果只記得一件事，請記得：「真皮」兩個字，喺呢個行業裡面幾乎冇資訊量。

講法實際係乜會變成點
頭層皮 full-grain最外層，粒面完整保留，唔打磨養成最靚，亦最貴、最易見到天然痕跡
二層皮 top-grain同一張皮，但粒面被磨平（或輕微修正），有時再壓花紋均勻、少瑕疵，但粒面已經冇咗，包漿弱
修正粒面 corrected grain瑕疵多嘅皮，磨到見肉再重壓一層假紋，通常加厚塗層開頭好靚，用落塗層先爆、先甩色
二層皮 split剖開後嘅下層，本來冇粒面，通常壓花＋上色耐磨一般，唔會養成
再生皮 bonded皮革碎料＋黏合劑壓成一塊，有時底層加布或 PU唔係「一層皮」，係一件複合材料

「真皮」、「優質真皮」呢類字眼，各國標示規定唔同，而且常常係法律戰場——監管機構多次就「bonded leather」同「100% leather」嘅描述採取行動。我唔會用呢啲詞，因為用咗就等於同客人玩一個佢玩唔贏嘅遊戲。

另外要講一樣好多人以為係保證嘅嘢：Leather Working Group（LWG）認證。LWG 係一個審核鞣革廠環境管理嘅國際體系，有金、白金等評級，係好事。但你要清楚佢審乜：佢審嘅係環境管理同追溯，唔係粒面品質，亦唔係產地。一間廠可以有 LWG 白金評級，同時出產二層皮。所以「LWG 認證」唔等於「頭層植鞣」，兩件事要分開問。

四、包漿究竟係乜：唔係迷信，係化學

「包漿」、「養皮」呢啲講法好玄，但拆開其實係幾種同時發生嘅化學同物理過程：

  • 單寧氧化同聚合。植鞣皮裡面殘留嘅單寧，長期接觸空氣同光，會慢慢氧化、聚合，顏色向紅棕、深咖啡方向走。呢個係最深層嘅變化，亦係植鞣皮獨有嘅色調。
  • 手油同皮脂滲入。你隻手每日分泌嘅油脂，會被纖維吸收，再慢慢氧化、聚合，令皮身變軟、變色、有光澤。邊位同手指最常接觸嘅位置會先深。
  • 紫外線。光會加速氧化，亦會令某些染料褪色。所以皮放喺窗邊會比放喺抽屜快變色。
  • 摩擦拋光（burnishing）。呢個係物理嘅：摩擦令粒面表層纖維壓實，反光變得均勻，就係嗰種「光澤」。呢個同塑膠嘅反光唔同，係纖維壓實之後嘅柔光。
  • 水分循環。吸濕、乾燥、再吸濕。呢個過程令皮身慢慢貼服你嘅手型。
  • 蠟同脂遷移。皮裡面上嘅油脂會向表面走，填平細紋。

呢度要修正一句我哋自己官網嘅講法。我哋寫「只有頭層皮會形成真正嘅包漿」。更準確嘅講法係：決定性因素係表面有冇一層塑膠膜，唔係單純鞣法。

因為大部分「便宜而有光澤」嘅皮，表面係噴咗一層聚氨酯／丙烯酸塗層（pigmented finish）。呢層膜令皮防水、防污、顏色一致——聽落好好。但佢亦令皮唔透氣、唔吸油：你用一年，深色嘅係嗰層膜上嘅污垢，而且最先壞嘅係層膜——爆裂、甩色、起皮。呢個就係「用耐咗變舊」同「養成」嘅分別。

相反，苯染（aniline）或半苯染嘅皮，染料滲入纖維、表面幾乎冇塗層，油脂同空氣可以入去，所以會養成。呢種皮唔一定係植鞣——例如 Horween 嘅 Chromexcel 係鉻鞣加植鞣復鞣，一樣養得好靚。但因為植鞣皮嘅賣點本身就係手感同時間，所以佢最典型。一句話：想要包漿，就要冇膜；你唔可以又要防水防污，又要會養成。

五、六個月同三年：實際會變成點

以下係經驗描述，唔係實驗數據。你可以當成「如果每日正常使用，大概會見到乜」。

大約六個月

  • 顏色由原本嘅原色／淺棕，轉向蜜糖色、淺咖啡。深淺唔平均：邊位、四角、同你手指成日摸到嘅位置會深一級。
  • 表面開始有一層很薄嘅柔光，唔係塑膠嘅反光，係纖維壓實之後嗰種。
  • 會出現幾條深色嘅手油痕，通常沿住你揸機嘅手指位置。
  • 可能有幾道淺刮痕，但被油填過，變成紋理嘅一部分，唔再刺眼。

大約三年

  • 整體深一至兩個色階。原色棕可能變成深咖啡帶紅；黑色會變得有層次，睇落唔再係一片死黑，而係見到光澤同紋理。
  • 邊位深到接近黑，角位可能有一點磨白，呢個係最真實嘅使用紀錄。
  • 皮身變軟、更貼服，但唔會鬆散——只要你有保養。
  • 如果你三年冇上過一次保養膏，會係另一個故事：乾、硬、細裂紋，尤其喺經常彎曲嘅位置。皮唔會自己保濕，呢個係你嘅責任，唔係皮嘅責任。

我哋官網喺保養欄寫嘅係「每 3 至 6 個月上一次保養膏，避免長時間陽光直射」。呢句唔係客套，係上面呢段時間線嘅操作條件。

六、老實講缺點：七樣你要接受嘅事

我唔會用「天然材質嘅特性」呢種說法去包裝缺點。直接列。

一、水漬

植鞣皮吸水，而水會將皮裡面嘅單寧同油脂推向表面。結果就係水痕：一撻深色，邊界清晰，乾咗都唔會完全消失。落雨、洗手、飲品濺到，都有機會。呢個係植鞣皮最實際嘅缺點，冇任何話術可以繞過。

二、紫外線會褪色同抽乾

長時間曝曬會加速氧化、亦令皮變乾。放喺車頭或者窗邊，你會見到顏色走樣。

三、要保養

三至六個月一次保養膏，香港嘅話可能要密啲。唔做嘅話，皮會乾、會裂。想要零維護嘅人，唔應該買皮。

四、香港嘅潮濕同發霉

我住喺香港，夏天相對濕度長期 80% 以上。皮係吸濕材料，長期收埋喺密閉、唔通風嘅地方（例如一個唔透氣嘅膠袋），有機會發霉。處理方法係保持通風、定期拎出嚟用、必要時用乾布抹。呢點好少人講，但對於華南嘅氣候係真實問題。

五、刮痕同染色轉移

刮痕會有，唔會消失，只可以拋光到唔顯眼。另外，深色皮（尤其新嘅）有機會將顏色轉移到淺色衣物，或者淺色皮吸收牛仔褲嘅藍。皮會記錄你去過邊。有人覺得浪漫，有人覺得污糟。

六、重量同厚度

皮革系列係 24 至 29 克、1.6 至 1.9 毫米。比芳綸（11 至 13 克、0.65 至 0.75 毫米）重一倍以上、厚兩倍以上。呢個係物理，唔係工藝問題。

七、同金屬接觸嘅腐蝕問題（技術上最麻煩嘅一項）

植鞣皮係偏酸性嘅材料——佢嘅單寧同殘留酸會同金屬反應。做刀鞘、做古董修復嘅人都知：植鞣皮長時間貼住鋼會令鋼生鏽，貼住銅、鎳合金會出現綠色嘅污跡（verdigris）。而手機殼裡面有磁石、可能有金屬件，手機本身亦係金屬中框。

所以呢件事唔可以當冇：磁石陣列唔應該直接貼住植鞣皮，中間一定要有阻隔層。呢個唔係「可以做好啲」嘅選項，係設計上必須解決嘅約束。呢個亦係點解皮革殼係「皮包覆內框」而唔係「一塊皮＋幾粒磁石」——中間嗰層結構，唔係為咗好看。

七、點解係 1.6 至 1.9 毫米

皮革厚度嘅行業單位係「盎司」（oz）：1 oz = 1/64 吋 ≈ 0.4 毫米。所以：

用途常見厚度換算 oz
銀包、卡片套內層0.8 – 1.2 mm約 2 – 3 oz
皮帶3.5 – 4.5 mm約 9 – 11 oz
錶帶2 – 3 mm約 5 – 7.5 oz
我哋嘅殼1.6 – 1.9 mm約 4 – 4.75 oz

呢個帶係取捨出嚟嘅：

  • 薄過大約 1.2 毫米：皮冇足夠纖維密度，包住內框嘅時候會被拉長，用落會鬆、會變形，邊位容易磨穿露出內框。做到薄，就係犧牲壽命。
  • 厚過大約 2 毫米：手機殼嘅四角係細圓角，厚皮包一個細半徑嘅彎，纖維外側會被拉伸、內側會被壓縮——唔削薄（skiving）就包唔服，硬包就會起皺甚至裂。削薄嘅位置係應力集中點，係最易爛嘅地方。同時重量同整體厚度亦會上去。
  • 帶內嘅取捨：1.6 毫米好包、手感薄，但抗磨同抗水差啲；1.9 毫米紮實、耐用，但重啲，轉角位要削薄處理。

所以我哋唔會講「越厚越好」，亦唔會講「最薄最好」。呢個帶係「包得服」同「用得耐」之間嘅平衡點。任何一個做過皮嘅人，都會同意冇一個厚度可以同時滿足兩邊。

八、你自己驗得到嘅方法（同三個唔可靠嘅方法）

可靠嘅檢查

  • 睇粒面紋路有冇重複。天然粒面唔會重複——毛孔分佈隨機、粗細唔均、有蚊叮同生長紋。壓花皮係用同一塊模具壓出嚟，你會搵到同一個紋樣出現兩次。呢個係最快嘅判斷。
  • 用手指壓落去，睇摺痕。頭層皮受壓會起細密、呈輻射狀嘅皺紋（好似你手指關節嘅皮）；修正粒面或再生皮會起寬闊、平滑、塑膠感嘅摺。
  • 睇邊位嘅剖面。頭層皮剖開係實心嘅纖維結構，表面有一層薄薄嘅粒面；再生皮會見到夾層、膠芯，或者底面貼咗一層布／PU。呢個係最難造假的證據。
  • 聞。真皮有味，係皮嘅味；重塗層或再生皮多數有膠味、塑膠味。
  • 睇內面同摺位嘅顏色一致性。如果表面有塗層，摺位同刮到嘅位置會露出下面唔同色嘅底層——呢個就係「膜」嘅證據。

唔可靠嘅方法（好多人信）

  • 滴水測試。水滴落去，如果吸咗、變深色，證明表面冇塑膠塗層——呢個係表面處理資訊，唔係「係唔係頭層」嘅證據。二層皮一樣可以做到吸水。而且呢個測試會留一個永久水印，請喺唔顯眼嘅位置做，或者唔做。
  • 睇價錢。貴唔一定係頭層植鞣。價錢反映品牌、渠道同故事，唔只係材料。
  • 燒。唔好燒。燒真皮同燒人造皮嘅味有分別，但需要經驗，而且你只會得到一個爛殼。

九、鞣完之後仲有好多步：「植鞣」兩個字唔係全部

呢一節係我買皮嘅時候真正會問嘅嘢。因為「vegetable-tanned」只係講咗鞣劑，冇講之後發生咩事。

一張皮鞣完，仲要經過：

  • 復鞣（retanning）。好多「植鞣皮」其實係先植鞣、再用合成鞣劑或樹脂復鞣，去改善手感、均勻度同耐水性。呢個唔一定係壞事，但你要知道：「植鞣」唔等於「100% 只用植物單寧」。如果有人咁講，問佢攞成分表。
  • 加脂（fatliquoring）。加動物油、植物油或合成油，決定皮嘅軟硬同耐乾裂能力。呢一步直接影響「養成」效果——加脂不足嘅皮，養到某個程度就會乾、會裂。
  • 乾燥同回濕。乾得太快會令纖維收縮不均，皮身變硬、變脆。
  • 表面處理（finishing）。呢一步決定佢係「同你一起老」定係「三年後爆塗層」。可以做苯染（幾乎冇膜）、半苯染、或者噴厚厚嘅顏料塗層。厚塗層可以遮蓋所有瑕疵，係便宜皮嘅好朋友，亦係包漿嘅敵人。
  • 分層同削薄（splitting & skiving）。一張皮本來有 4 至 6 毫米以上，要剖到你要嘅厚度，並喺摺位削薄。

所以當你問「呢塊係唔係植鞣皮」，正確嘅追問係：「係純植鞣定係植鞣復鞣？表面係苯染定係塗層？厚度公差幾多？」呢三條問題，一個認真做皮嘅供應商答得出。

十、保養實際點做（同水痕點救）

官網寫「每 3 至 6 個月上一次保養膏」，但冇寫點上。呢度補完：

  • 先抹乾淨、先抹乾。塵同沙會喺上油嘅時候被推入纖維，等於用砂紙磨自己塊皮。
  • 少量、分次。用指腹或軟布取極少份量，薄薄推開，等佢吸收。一次落太多，皮會變得油膩、顏色會不均，而且會黏塵。
  • 先喺唔顯眼嘅位置試。保養膏會令顏色變深（所有油都係咁），先喺內側試一細塊，睇下你接唔接受。
  • 等佢乾透先落袋。唔好上完油即刻放落袋，會壓出油印。
  • 避免含溶劑、含矽嘅產品。呢類產品會封住表面，短期好靚，長期令皮唔透氣、變乾。用返最基本嘅天然蠟／油脂配方就夠。

至於水痕——先講一句老實話：水痕冇得「完全消除」，只可以「整到唔覺眼」。

  • 第一步：即刻吸。用乾布或紙巾輕壓吸水，唔好捽（捽會擴大範圍）。
  • 第二步：唔好用風筒吹。高溫會令皮收縮、變硬，水痕反而固定。
  • 第三步：自然陰乾，然後整片上油。水痕之所以顯眼，係因為嗰個位置嘅油脂被水推走咗。做法係：將嗰一撻嘅油推去整塊皮——即係成塊皮上一層薄油，令色差由「一撻」變成「整體深淺漸變」。呢個係最有效嘅方法。
  • 第四步：接受。有啲水痕永遠都會見到少少。呢個係你嘅紀錄。

十一、植鞣皮係唔係「環保皮」？

呢個問題好多人問，而我唔想答得太靚。

冇一種皮係純粹環保嘅。皮係肉類工業嘅副產品——養牛嘅土地、水、飼料同甲烷排放，唔應該由一塊手機殼承擔，但如果牛皮唔做皮，佢就係廢物。所以比較公平嘅講法係：「用咗本來會被丟棄嘅一部分」，而唔係「拯救咗地球」。

植鞣嘅好處係唔涉及鉻，但佢有自己嘅成本：植鞣時間長（幾星期對幾小時）、耗水、需要大片土地種單寧植物（栗木、含羞草、白堅木），而傳統植鞣廠嘅廢水雖然唔含鉻，但有機物同顏色負荷好高。

所以我唔會講「植鞣＝環保」。正確嘅講法係：植鞣用另一種代價，換另一種效果。如果你真係在意環境，最有效嘅問題唔係「植鞣定鉻鞣」，而係——「呢間鞣革廠係唔係 LWG 審核過、評級幾多？」因為決定污染嘅，係廠嘅管理水平，唔係鞣劑嘅名字。

十二、我最後點解仍然揀佢

睇到呢度，你可能會問：又要保養、又怕水、又會變色、又會發霉——點解唔用一個乾淨俐落嘅塑膠殼？

因為塑膠殼三年之後，同第一日一樣，只係污糟咗。而皮殼三年之後，係一件只屬於你嘅物件。呢個唔係浪漫，係功能：呢個係唯一一種會把你嘅使用行為記錄落去嘅材料。你揸邊個位、放邊個袋、去過邊度，全部寫喺上面。

我做外貿二十六年，賣過好多「一次性」嘅產品：用完即棄、壞咗就換、三年之後完全冇痕跡。手機殼大部分都係呢種。所以當我睇到一塊材料，唯一嘅賣點係「時間」嘅時候，我停低咗。

我投資嘅 HANDSOME 三個系列裡面，皮革係定價最低嘅一個（US$29–39），但係我最想留低嘅一個。

十三、三句話總結

第一句：植鞣同鉻鞣係兩種答案。鉻鞣答「效率同穩定」，植鞣答「性格同時間」。冇一邊係「假皮」。

第二句：包漿嘅關鍵唔係鞣法，係表面有冇一層塑膠膜。想要會養成嘅皮，就要接受佢怕水、要保養、會留痕。

第三句：呢塊皮唔啱所有人。想要零維護、想要防水防污、想要每次睇落都一模一樣嘅人，請買芳綸或者防摔——嗰兩個系列係為你而做嘅。呢個唔係趕客，係我唔想聽你半年後投訴。

Twenty-six years in product, and the first time someone suggested "let's pick a leather that changes colour", my reaction was the same as most people's: so there's something wrong with it?

The common sense of buying things is that nothing should change. White should stay white. Black should stay black. Any material that changes colour is usually unstable, or ageing badly.

Leather is the exception. And not just an exception — changing is its only selling point, and the only reason I chose it in the end.

This piece covers what vegetable-tanned leather is, what patina actually is as chemistry, what it looks like at six months and at three years, and most importantly — how many downsides it has, and who should not buy it. If you finish this and decide it isn't for you, I'll consider the article a success.

1. First, what "leather" actually is

Cut a cowhide open and you get several layers:

  • Epidermis and hair — the outside. Removed before tanning. No use to us.
  • The grain layer — the outermost part of the dermis, where the hair follicles and pores sit. The fibres here are the finest and tightest, and the natural markings live here. When people say "grain", this is the layer they mean.
  • The corium (reticular dermis) — the layer below, a woven network of coarse collagen fibre bundles. This supplies strength and most of the thickness.
  • The flesh side — the bottom, loose fibres.

"Full-grain" means the piece that keeps the grain layer — the outermost slice, unsanded, with the grain intact. Precisely because nothing has been sanded away, it keeps the natural marks: insect bites, scratches, growth lines, and the difference between tight fibre at the neck and looser fibre at the belly. Those are not defects. They are the hide's identity card. If you want every piece to look identical, what you actually want is plastic.

Split a hide horizontally and you get "split leather" — the corium, with no grain at all. That is the key to everything that follows about "top-grain", "corrected grain" and "bonded".

2. Vegetable tanning and chrome tanning: two chemistries, two temperaments

Raw hide is protein: it rots, and it dries into a hard board. To become leather it has to be tanned — chemically stabilised so that it neither rots nor shrinks into a lump when wet. History gave us two main routes.

Vegetable tanning: plant polyphenols, absorbed slowly

Vegetable tanning uses tannins, which are plant polyphenols. There are two broad families:

  • Hydrolysable tannins — oak gall, chestnut, tara, myrobalan. Smaller molecules, so they penetrate deep into the hide.
  • Condensed tannins — mimosa, quebracho, gambier. Larger molecules, mostly responsible for the fullness and colour of the surface.

They don't simply "wrap" the fibres. They hydrogen-bond to the amide groups and peptide bonds of the collagen, and lock the fibre bundles to each other. Untanned fibres slide and swell with water; locked fibres are stable. The process is slow: traditionally the hides move through a series of pits and drums, from weak to strong liquor, over several weeks. That is why vegetable-tanned leather comes out a warm yellow-tan colour, with a firm, substantial hand — good for carving, embossing and punching — and keeps darkening with light and time.

Chrome tanning: chromium(III), done in hours

Chrome tanning uses basic chromium(III) sulfate. The Cr(III) ions coordinate with the carboxyl groups of the collagen, forming crosslinks. The reaction is far quicker: hours rather than weeks.

Chrome tanning's advantages are real, and I won't run it down just to sell vegetable-tanned leather:

  • Much better heat resistance. Chrome-tanned leather shrinks at 100 °C and above; vegetable-tanned shrinks in the 70 °C class. So chrome-tanned is better for processes involving hot water, steaming or moulding.
  • Better water resistance, more uniform, softer. It dyes to bright colours and scales consistently.
  • Faster and cheaper. Which is why it accounts for more than 80% of world leather production. Most of the leather you own is chrome-tanned.

The downsides matter too: chromium-bearing wastewater is a genuine environmental problem, and whether a tannery treats it properly decides whether this leather is "clean". And two things need separating: trivalent chromium (Cr(III)) and hexavalent chromium (Cr(VI)) are not the same substance. Cr(VI) is a carcinogen and heavily regulated; Cr(III) is far less toxic and is the form the leather industry actually uses. But under the wrong conditions — certain oxidising environments, or incinerating waste — Cr(III) can convert to Cr(VI). So this is not a non-issue, and "chrome-tanned means toxic leather" is not honest either. Both slogans are lazy.

3. Full-grain, top-grain, corrected grain, bonded: the vocabulary game

If you remember one thing from this section, make it this: the words "genuine leather" carry almost no information in this industry.

TermWhat it actually isWhat happens to it
Full-grainOutermost slice, grain fully intact, unsandedPatina best; also most expensive and most likely to show natural marks

Words like "genuine leather" and "premium genuine leather" are labelled differently in different countries, and they are often a legal battleground — regulators have repeatedly acted over how "bonded leather" and "100% leather" are described. I don't use those words, because using them means playing a game the customer cannot win.

One more thing that many people treat as a guarantee: Leather Working Group (LWG) certification. LWG is an international system that audits tanneries on environmental management, with ratings such as Gold and Platinum. That is a good thing. But be clear about what it audits: environmental management and traceability — not grain quality, and not country of origin. A tannery can hold LWG Gold and still produce split leather. "LWG certified" is not the same as "full-grain, vegetable-tanned". Ask them separately.

4. What patina actually is: not mysticism, chemistry

"Patina" and "developing the leather" sound mystical, but take them apart and they are several chemical and physical processes running at once:

  • Oxidation and polymerisation of tannins. Residual tannins in vegetable-tanned leather slowly oxidise and polymerise in air and light, shifting the colour toward red-brown and deep coffee. This is the deepest change, and the tone that is specific to vegetable-tanned leather.
  • Skin oils and sebum soaking in. The oils your hands produce every day are absorbed into the fibres and then slowly oxidise and polymerise, softening the leather, deepening the colour and adding sheen. Edges and the spots your fingers touch most darken first.
  • Ultraviolet light. Light accelerates oxidation and fades some dyes. Leather left by a window changes faster than leather left in a drawer.
  • Friction burnishing. This one is physical: rubbing compacts the surface fibres of the grain so the reflection becomes even — that sheen. It is not the same as plastic's shine. It is a soft glow from compacted fibre.
  • Moisture cycling. Absorb, dry, absorb again. Over time it makes the leather conform to your hand.
  • Migration of waxes and fats. Oils in the leather travel toward the surface and fill fine lines.

Here I have to correct a line on our own site. We write that only full-grain develops a real patina. The more accurate statement is that the decisive factor is whether the surface carries a plastic film — not the tannage alone.

Most cheap, shiny leather is sprayed with a polyurethane or acrylic coating (a pigmented finish). That film makes the leather water-resistant, stain-resistant and colour-consistent, which sounds great. But it also makes the leather non-breathable and unable to absorb oils: after a year, what has darkened is dirt on the film, and the first thing to fail is the film itself — cracking, flaking, peeling. That is the difference between "wearing out" and "developing".

Conversely, aniline or semi-aniline leather — dye soaked into the fibre with almost no coating — lets oils and air in, so it develops. That leather is not necessarily vegetable-tanned: Horween's Chromexcel, for instance, is chrome-tanned with a vegetable re-tan, and it ages beautifully. But vegetable-tanned leather is the classic case, because its whole selling proposition is touch and time. In one line: if you want patina, you can't have a film; and you cannot have both stain-proofing and patina.

5. Six months and three years: what actually happens

What follows is experience, not test data. Read it as "with normal daily use, roughly this".

At about six months

  • The colour moves from its original tan toward honey and light coffee. It is uneven: edges, corners and the spots your fingers touch most are a shade darker.
  • A very thin soft sheen appears. Not a plastic reflection — the glow of compacted fibre.
  • A few dark finger-oil streaks show up, usually where you grip the phone.
  • There may be light scratches, but oil has filled them, so they read as part of the texture rather than as damage.

At about three years

  • One to two shades deeper overall. An original tan may become a deep reddish coffee; black gains depth — no longer a flat black but something with sheen and grain.
  • Edges go nearly black. Corners may show a little wear to a lighter tone. That is the most honest record of use you will get.
  • The leather is softer and conforms better, but it isn't floppy — as long as you've cared for it.
  • If you never conditioned it once in three years, it's a different story: dry, stiff, fine cracks, especially where it flexes. Leather does not moisturise itself. That's your job, not the leather's.

Our own site's care note says to condition every three to six months and keep it out of prolonged direct sun. That isn't politeness. It is the operating condition for the timeline above.

6. The honest downsides: seven things you have to accept

I won't dress these up as "the nature of a natural material". Here they are.

One: water marks

Vegetable-tanned leather absorbs water, and water pushes tannins and oils toward the surface. The result is a water mark: a darker patch with a sharp edge that does not fully disappear when it dries. Rain, hand-washing, a spilled drink — all can do it. This is the most practical downside of vegetable-tanned leather, and no amount of good writing gets around it.

Two: UV fades it and dries it out

Prolonged sun accelerates oxidation and dries the leather. Leave it on a dashboard or by a window and you will see the colour shift.

Three: it needs care

A conditioning balm every three to six months, possibly more often in Hong Kong. Skip it and the leather dries and cracks. If you want zero maintenance, don't buy leather.

Four: humidity and mould in Hong Kong

I live in Hong Kong. In summer, relative humidity sits above 80% for months. Leather is hygroscopic, so stored long-term somewhere sealed and unventilated — a non-breathable plastic bag, say — it can grow mould. The fix is ventilation, taking it out and using it, and wiping with a dry cloth when needed. Few people mention this, but in a subtropical climate it is a real problem.

Five: scratches and colour transfer

Scratches happen and don't disappear; you can only burnish them until they're less obvious. Dark leather (especially new) can transfer colour to light clothing, and light leather absorbs indigo from jeans. Leather records where you have been. Some people find that romantic. Some find it dirty.

Six: weight and thickness

The leather range is 24 to 29 g and 1.6 to 1.9 mm — more than twice the weight and more than twice the thickness of the aramid range (11–13 g, 0.65–0.75 mm). That is physics, not a craft problem.

Seven: corrosion in contact with metal (the technically awkward one)

Vegetable-tanned leather is acidic: its tannins and residual acids react with metals. Anyone who makes knife sheaths or restores antiques knows this — vegetable-tanned leather left against steel rusts it, and against copper or nickel alloys it produces green staining (verdigris). A phone case contains magnets, possibly metal parts, and the phone itself has a metal frame.

So this cannot be waved away: the magnet array must not sit directly against vegetable-tanned leather. There has to be a barrier layer between them. That is not a "nice to have" — it is a design constraint that has to be solved. It is also why a leather case is "leather wrapped over a frame" rather than "a piece of leather plus a few magnets". The structure in between is not there to look good.

7. Why 1.6 to 1.9 mm

The industry unit for leather thickness is the ounce (oz): 1 oz = 1/64 inch ≈ 0.4 mm. So:

UseTypical thicknessIn ounces
Wallet and card holder interiors0.8 – 1.2 mmabout 2 – 3 oz
Belts3.5 – 4.5 mmabout 9 – 11 oz
Watch straps2 – 3 mmabout 5 – 7.5 oz
Our case1.6 – 1.9 mmabout 4 – 4.75 oz

That band is a trade-off:

  • Below about 1.2 mm: there isn't enough fibre density. Wrapped around the frame the leather stretches, then loosens and deforms in use, and the edges wear through and expose the frame. Making it thin means giving up lifespan.
  • Above about 2 mm: the corners of a phone case are tight radii. Wrapping thick leather around a small radius stretches the outside fibres and compresses the inside — without skiving it simply won't conform, and forcing it creases or cracks. The skived area is a stress concentration point and the weak spot. Weight and overall thickness climb as well.
  • Within the band: 1.6 mm wraps easily and feels slim, but resists abrasion and water less well. 1.9 mm is substantial and durable, but heavier, and the corners need skiving.

So we won't say "thicker is better", and we won't say "thinnest is best". This band is the balance point between wrapping cleanly and lasting. Anyone who has actually worked leather will agree that no single thickness satisfies both.

8. Checks you can run yourself (and three that don't work)

Checks that work

  • Look for a repeating grain pattern. Natural grain never repeats: pores are randomly distributed, uneven in size, with insect bites and growth lines. Embossed leather comes off one mould, so you will find the same mark twice. This is the fastest test there is.
  • Press it with a thumb and watch the creases. Full-grain raises fine, radiating wrinkles — like the skin over your knuckle. Corrected grain or bonded leather makes broad, smooth, plastic-looking folds.
  • Look at the cut edge in cross-section. Full-grain cuts as a solid fibre structure with a thin grain layer on top. Bonded leather shows a sandwich, a glued core, or a fabric or PU backing. This is the hardest evidence to fake.
  • Smell it. Real leather smells of leather. Heavily coated or bonded material mostly smells of glue and plastic.
  • Check colour consistency between the surface, the folds and the underside. If there is a coating, folds and scuffed spots will reveal a different-coloured layer underneath. That is the film showing itself.

Checks that don't work (and that many people trust)

  • The water drop test. If a drop soaks in and darkens the leather, there is no plastic coating — that tells you about the surface finish, not about whether it's full-grain. Split leather can be made to absorb water too. And the test leaves a permanent mark, so do it somewhere hidden, or not at all.
  • Judging by price. Expensive is not automatically full-grain vegetable-tanned. Price reflects brand, channel and story, not only material.
  • Burning it. Don't. Real and synthetic leather do smell different when burned, but reading it takes experience, and all you get is a ruined case.

9. Tanning is not the end of it: "vegetable-tanned" is not the whole story

This section is what I actually ask when I buy leather. Because "vegetable-tanned" tells you about the tanning agent and nothing about what happens afterwards.

After tanning, a hide still goes through:

  • Retanning. Many "vegetable-tanned" hides are vegetable-tanned first and then retanned with syntans or resins to improve hand, uniformity and water resistance. That isn't automatically bad, but you should know: "vegetable-tanned" does not mean "100% plant tannins only". If someone claims that, ask for the composition.
  • Fatliquoring. Animal, vegetable or synthetic oils are added, and this decides how soft the leather is and how well it resists drying out and cracking. It also directly affects how it ages — leather that is under-fatliquored will dry and crack partway through its patina.
  • Drying and re-wetting. Drying too fast makes the fibres shrink unevenly, and the leather turns hard and brittle.
  • Finishing. This decides whether the leather ages with you or cracks its coating in three years. Options run from aniline (almost no film) through semi-aniline to a heavy pigmented spray. A thick coating hides every defect — a great friend to cheap leather, and the enemy of patina.
  • Splitting and skiving. A hide starts out 4–6 mm or more thick. It has to be split down to the thickness you want, and skived thin at the folds.

So when you ask "is this vegetable-tanned?", the useful follow-ups are: "Pure vegetable-tanned, or vegetable with a retan? Aniline or coated? What is the thickness tolerance?" A serious leather supplier can answer all three.

10. How to actually care for it (and how to rescue a water mark)

Our site says to condition every three to six months, but it doesn't say how. Here is the rest.

  • Clean and dry it first. Dust and grit get pushed into the fibres when you apply balm. That is sandpapering your own leather.
  • Little and often. Take a tiny amount on a fingertip or soft cloth, spread it thin, let it absorb. Too much at once makes leather greasy, uneven in colour, and a dust magnet.
  • Test somewhere hidden first. Balm darkens leather — all oils do. Try a small patch on the inside and see whether you accept the colour.
  • Let it dry properly before it goes back in a pocket. Otherwise you press oil marks into it.
  • Avoid solvent-based and silicone products. They look great short-term by sealing the surface, and long-term they suffocate and dry the leather. A basic natural wax and oil formula is enough.

As for water marks — the honest version first: a water mark cannot be fully removed. It can only be made inconspicuous.

  • Step one: blot immediately. Press a dry cloth or tissue to absorb it. Don't rub — rubbing spreads it.
  • Step two: don't use a hairdryer. Heat shrinks and hardens leather and fixes the mark in place.
  • Step three: air-dry naturally, then oil the whole panel. A water mark stands out because the oils were pushed out of that spot. The fix is to spread that spot's oil across the whole piece — a thin coat over the entire surface, turning a patch into an overall gradient. This is the method that actually works.
  • Step four: accept it. Some marks stay faintly visible forever. That is your record.

11. Is vegetable-tanned leather "eco leather"?

People ask this a lot, and I don't want to give a flattering answer.

No leather is purely environmental. Leather is a by-product of the meat industry — the land, water, feed and methane of cattle farming shouldn't be loaded onto a phone case, but if the hide isn't made into leather it is waste. The fair statement is therefore: "this uses a part that would otherwise be thrown away" — not "this saves the planet".

Vegetable tanning's advantage is that it involves no chromium, but it has its own costs: it takes weeks instead of hours, it uses a lot of water, it needs land to grow tannin plants (chestnut, mimosa, quebracho), and while vegetable tannery effluent contains no chromium, its organic and colour load is very high.

So I won't say "vegetable-tanned = green". The accurate statement is: vegetable tanning pays a different price for a different result. If you genuinely care about the environment, the most useful question isn't "vegetable or chrome?" It is "is this tannery LWG audited, and at what rating?" Because pollution is decided by how a factory is managed, not by the name of the tanning agent.

12. Why I still chose it

By now you might ask: it needs care, it fears water, it changes colour, it can grow mould — why not just use a clean, simple plastic case?

Because after three years, a plastic case looks exactly as it did on day one, only dirtier. A leather case after three years is an object that belongs only to you. That isn't romance; it's function. It is the only material here that writes down how you used it. Where you grip, which pocket it lives in, where you've been — all of it is on the surface.

Twenty-six years in export, I've sold a lot of disposable products: use it, break it, replace it, and after three years there is no trace. Most phone cases are that. So when I found a material whose only selling point is time, I stopped.

Of the three ranges at HANDSOME, the brand I invested in, leather is the cheapest (US$29–39) — and the one I most want to keep.

13. Three sentences to finish

One: Vegetable and chrome tanning are two answers. Chrome answers efficiency and consistency; vegetable answers character and time. Neither is "fake leather".

Two: What decides patina is not the tannage but whether the surface carries a plastic film. If you want leather that develops, you accept that it fears water, needs care, and keeps a record.

Three: This leather is not for everyone. If you want zero maintenance, water and stain resistance, and the same look every time, buy aramid or the drop-protection range — those two were made for you. That's not me turning away business. It's me not wanting your complaint in six months.
