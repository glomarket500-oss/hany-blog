模型越賣越便宜，AI 的債越滾越大Models Keep Getting Cheaper While AI's Debt Keeps Ballooning

2026年11月16日Nov 16, 2026 週更觀察Weekly Notes 粵港澳・全國Greater Bay Area & nationwide
【鄭哥週評】欄目：鄭哥博客週評｜日期：2026-11-16

十一月呢個星期，我讀晨報讀到一個好奇怪嘅畫面。一邊，DeepSeek 把成個 App 攤開做 API，搜索同抓取五蚊一千次，語音識別五折後 0.39 蚊一個鐘，連可以導入歷史對話嘅前端 Demo 都開源埋。另一邊，美聯儲喺半年度《金融穩定報告》裏面，第一次逐項點名 AI 嘅債——6800 億美元債券、1.1 萬億未償還債務、3 萬億美元遠期租賃同兜底擔保，87% 受訪者話 AI 係金融系統「顯著風險」，而春天調查嗰陣只得 50%。 模型越嚟越便宜，賬單越嚟越貴。呢種「平」同「貴」同時出現嘅局面，上一年我哋係見唔到嘅——以前 AI 嘅故事只有一個方向，就係「以後會更好」。呢個星期，有人開始反問：好咗之後，邊個埋單？我判斷呢個星期嘅主題，唔係 AI 能做乜，係 AI 嘅錢由邊度嚟、又由邊個還。

一、央行第一次翻開 AI 嘅賬本

美聯儲呢份報告嘅份量，唔喺任何一個單獨數字，喺佢終於把表外嗰層紙戳開。報告寫明：截至十月，先進計算相關債券發行已經超過 6800 億美元——舊年全年先至 1300 億，一年膨脹咗四倍有多；未償還公共債務超過 1.1 萬億；數據中心嘅遠期租賃同兜底擔保，估算約莫 3 萬億美元。呢個 3 萬億唔係已經借咗嘅錢，係承諾咗但未上表嘅錢。最刺眼嘅係個情緒數字：87% 受訪者把 AI 列為金融體系「顯著風險」，春天調查嗰陣只得 50%。

我判斷呢段話講嘅其實係一件事：GPU 殘值擔保同長約租賃互相套住，平時唔入資產負債表，一跌就一齊爆。市場過去一年計 AI 嘅賬，計嘅係營收增速——邊個收入翻咗一倍，邊個訂單積壓過千億；央行而家計嘅係另一本賬，計嘅係違約嗰陣邊個執笠、邊個攞唔返押金。兩本賬嘅結論可以完全相反：一間公司可以收入爆升，同時距離爆煲只差一次利率上調。

來源：新浪財經《美聯儲報告：「AI 熱」正推高資產估值 或對金融系統構成風險》，2026-11-14｜https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2026-11-14/doc-iniysrax0322040.shtml

二、回調開始收人命，由基金開始

債係央行睇嘅，基金清盤係散戶實實在在感受到嘅。長盛成長精選因為連續五十個工作日淨值低過五千萬，進入清算程序；佢三季度頭兩大重倉，係北方華創 7.99%、盛科通信 7.13%，今年累計虧咗 33.27%。同一星期，中銀成長優選、博時優質精選呢批同類基金，先後發出清盤預警。

把呢條線同英偉達嗰邊連埋睇，會更清楚：黃仁勳辭任之後，英偉達本週股價跌咗 13%，市值蒸發 5730 億美元。我判斷呢兩件事係同一個螺旋嘅兩面——跌，規模縮；縮，贖回；贖回，再跌。被動賣出會放大跌幅，同基本面好唔好無關。

仲有一個日子要 mark 低：英偉達三季報喺 17 日盤後出，市場畀嘅營收指引係 1080 億美元，預期同比增約莫 91%。我判斷呢一刻先至係真正嘅定價點——股價跌咗 13% 之後，市場其實一直喺度等一個數字，睇吓 AI 嘅需求係咪真係對得上嗰 6800 億嘅債。對得上，回調就係洗盤；對唔上，呢個螺旋先至開始。

來源：鳳凰網《重倉 AI 硬件，一批基金瀕臨清盤》，2026-11-16｜https://finance.ifeng.com/c/8y4dUeSgIl9

三、卡買得到，電同牆買唔到

錢之外，仲有另一重帳。三星據報洽購美國數據中心冷卻商 Modine，後者市值約莫 56 億美元，大金、LG、維諦都喺候場；Cerebras 聯席 CEO 都承認，佢哋嘅數據中心建設出現咗延遲（財聯社）。

我判斷呢兩單新聞講緊同一句：芯片可以落單、機房可以租，但電網同施工隊冇得加急。AI 基建嘅瓶頸，正由「有冇卡」變成「有冇電、有冇牆、有冇散熱」。

呢種「軟硬件錯位」唔係今個星期先有，但今個星期開始有公司用真金白銀投票——三星要買嘅係一間市值 56 億美元嘅散熱廠，而唔係任何一間 GPU 公司。我判斷呢一步棋講嘅係：晶片嘅邊際成本喺跌，電同散熱嘅邊際成本喺升，邊一樣貴，錢就流向邊一樣。

來源：搜狐《三星電子據悉洽購數據中心冷卻設備生產商摩丁製造》，2026-11-14｜https://www.sohu.com/a/1100878078_222256

四、模型平咗，貴嘅另有其人

咁模型嗰邊呢？十一月十三日晚，DeepSeek 開放「全家桶應用 API」：搜索、抓取五蚊千次，語音識別限時五折 0.39 蚊一個鐘，連可以導入歷史對話嘅前端 Demo 都開源埋。Intel 嗰邊，Crescent Island 訂單超過 20 億美元，成為 Intel 史上最暢銷顯示卡，靠嘅係唔碰 HBM、改用 LPDDR5X。

我判斷呢兩單嘢係同一件事嘅兩面：模型能力同推理硬件都喺度「去貴嘢」——API 按次計費越打越低，顯卡繞開最貴嘅 HBM。平嘅係模型同卡，貴嘅係圍住佢哋嘅債、電、散熱。做應用嘅人如果以為「模型平咗等於成本平咗」，會計錯呢條數。

Intel 嗰張卡嘅關鍵唔喺訂單數字，喺佢「砍」咗啲乜：公版 160GB、可定制到 480GB，單卡扛得起 3000 億參數，仲徹底砍掉顯示輸出——佢係一張純為推理而生嘅卡。我判斷呢個先係「去貴嘢」嘅真正含義：當 HBM 貴到做推理唔划算，市場自然會生出一條唔用 HBM 嘅路。

來源：IT之家《DeepSeek 發佈全家桶應用 API：開發者將能 1:1 複刻完整 DeepSeek App》，2026-11-14｜https://www.ithome.com/1/026/960.htm

五、鄭哥週評

落到我自己——做脣釉半成品同料體出海，呢條線本週無直接關係。我嘅成本喺化工原料同人工，唔喺算力。但呢份報告教咗我一樣嘢：景氣分兩層，訂單同回款係真嘅，賬單遲早要算。我接單時睇嘅唔應該淨係出貨價，仲有客戶嘅回款賬期。

我判斷 AI 呢一輪唔會因為一份報告就停，但「算賬」會由央行個別報告，變成每一家基金、每一條產線嘅日常。以前講故事嘅人贏，而家識計數嘅人接手；泡沫少咗，真正交到貨嘅人先企得穩。

\[Zheng Ge's Weekly Take\] Column: Zheng Ge's weekly review | Date: 2026-11-16

This week I read the morning briefs and came across a strange picture. On one side, DeepSeek opened up its entire app as an API — search and scraping at 5 yuan per thousand calls, speech recognition at 0.39 yuan an hour after a limited-time half-price cut, and even the front-end demo that can import conversation history was open-sourced. On the other, the Federal Reserve's semi-annual Financial Stability Report named AI's debts one by one — over $680 billion in bonds, more than $1.1 trillion in outstanding debt, roughly $3 trillion in data-center forward leases and backstop guarantees, and 87% of respondents calling AI a "notable risk" to the financial system, versus just 50% in the spring survey. Models keep getting cheaper; the bill keeps getting bigger. This "cheap" and "expensive" happening at the same time is something we did not see last year — before, AI's story had only one direction: "it will get better." This week, someone started asking back: after it gets better, who pays? My judgment is that this week's theme is not what AI can do, but where AI's money comes from and who pays it back.

The Central Bank Opens AI's Books for the First Time

The weight of the Fed's report is not in any single number, but in finally puncturing the off-balance-sheet layer. The report states that advanced-computing-related bond issuance had already topped $680 billion by October — the full-year figure was just $130 billion last year, a more than fourfold expansion in one year; outstanding public debt exceeded $1.1 trillion; and data-center forward leases and backstop guarantees are estimated at roughly $3 trillion. Note that this $3 trillion is not money already borrowed, but money promised yet not on the balance sheet. The sharpest number is the sentiment one: 87% of respondents called AI a "notable risk" to the financial system, versus just 50% in the spring survey.

My judgment is that this says one thing: GPU residual-value guarantees and long-term leases are wrapped around each other, normally kept off the balance sheet, and they all blow up together when the fall comes. Over the past year the market accounted for AI by revenue growth — who doubled revenue, whose backlog passed a hundred billion; the central bank is now accounting by a different book — who goes under in a default, and who cannot get their deposits back. The two books can reach completely opposite conclusions: a company can be exploding in revenue while standing one rate hike away from a blow-up.

The Pullback Starts Taking Lives, Beginning With Funds

Debt is what the central bank watches; fund liquidation is what retail and institutional investors actually feel. Changsheng Growth Select entered liquidation because its net asset value stayed below 50 million yuan for 50 consecutive working days; its top two holdings last quarter were Naura Technology at 7.99% and Centec Communications at 7.13%, and it lost 33.27% this year. The same week, BOC Growth Select, Bosera Quality Select and a batch of similar funds issued liquidation warnings.

Link this to Nvidia and it gets clearer: after Jensen Huang's resignation, Nvidia's share price fell 13% this week, erasing $573 billion in market value. My judgment is that these two things are two sides of the same spiral — it falls, the size shrinks; it shrinks, redemptions come; redemptions come, it falls further. Forced selling amplifies the decline, and it has nothing to do with fundamentals.

There is one more date to mark: Nvidia's third-quarter results come out after the close on the 17th, with the market's revenue guidance at $108 billion, expected to grow about 91% year over year. My judgment is that this is the real pricing moment — after the 13% drop, the market has been waiting for one number to see whether AI demand really matches that $680 billion of debt. If it does, the pullback is a washout; if it does not, the spiral is only beginning.

Chips You Can Buy; Power and Walls You Cannot

Beyond money there is another ledger. Samsung is reportedly in talks to acquire Modine, a US data-center cooling maker valued at about $5.6 billion, with Daikin, LG and Vertiv also in the wings; Cerebras' co-CEO admitted that, like the rest of the industry, its data-center construction has slipped.

My judgment is that these two stories say the same thing: chips can be ordered and machine rooms can be rented, but power grids and construction crews cannot be rushed. The bottleneck in AI infrastructure is shifting from "do we have chips" to "do we have power, walls and cooling."

This hardware-software mismatch is not new, but this week companies started voting with real money — Samsung is buying a $5.6 billion cooling plant, not any GPU company. My judgment is that this move says: the marginal cost of chips is falling while the marginal cost of power and cooling is rising, and money flows to whichever one is getting expensive.

Models Got Cheaper — Something Else Got Pricier

And the model side? On the evening of 13 November, DeepSeek opened its "full-suite app API": search and scraping at 5 yuan per thousand calls, speech recognition at 0.39 yuan an hour after a limited-time half-price cut, and even the front-end demo that can import conversation history was open-sourced. On Intel's side, Crescent Island orders topped $2 billion, making it the best-selling graphics card in Intel's history — by skipping HBM and switching to LPDDR5X.

My judgment is that these two are two sides of the same thing: model capability and inference hardware are both "de-premiuming" — API pay-per-use keeps dropping, and the card routes around the most expensive HBM. What got cheap is models and cards; what got expensive is the debt, power and cooling wrapped around them. Anyone building applications who thinks "models got cheaper, so my costs got cheaper" has done the math wrong.

The key to Intel's card is not the order number but what it cut: 160GB in the base version, configurable up to 480GB, a single card able to carry 300 billion parameters, and the display output removed entirely — meaning it is a card built purely for inference, with no interest in pleasing gamers. My judgment is that this is the real meaning of "de-premiuming": when HBM gets too expensive for inference to make sense, the market naturally grows a route that skips HBM.

5. Zheng Ge's Weekly Take

For my own business — making lip gloss semi-finished products and formulations for export — this week's thread has no direct connection. My costs sit in chemical raw materials and labour, not in compute; AI's debt is several layers away from me. But the Fed's report taught me one thing: every boom has two layers — orders are real, payments are real, but the bill always comes due. When I take an order I should look not just at the ex-factory price but at the customer's payment terms; AI's current trouble is exactly that borrowed money is starting to mature, and that lesson applies to any business.

My judgment is that this AI cycle will not stop because of one report, but settling accounts will shift from a central bank's occasional report to the daily routine of every fund and every production line. The storytellers used to win; now the people who can count are taking over. Once the price tag is on, money no longer gets cheaper for free — and for people running real businesses, that is actually good news: less froth means only those who actually deliver will stand firm.
