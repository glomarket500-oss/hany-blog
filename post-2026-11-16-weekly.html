嘴上喊威脅，開源賬單89%付給中國Threat on the lips, 89% of open-source bills paid to China

2026年11月16日Nov 16, 2026 週更觀察Weekly Notes 粵港澳・全國Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao and nationwide
【鄭哥週評】欄目：鄭哥博客週評｜日期：2026-11-16

呢個星期我睇住晨報，有一條線反反覆覆咁出現：美國一邊把中國 AI 講成頭號威脅，一邊把開源模型嘅賬單八九成咁付畀中國公司。同一個禮拜，DeepSeek 把成個 App 開成 API，美團把 1.6 萬億參數嘅模型權重成張枱送出去，百度仲把 Token 擺上咗櫃檯。三件事擺埋一齊睇，唔似巧合，似係同一套打法分三個方向落地。 呢三件事加埋，就係我今次要寫嘅判斷：模型嘅戰場，已經由「邊個模型叻」變成「邊個揸住地基同計價單位」。模型愈平，地基之爭就愈狠。呢句我上個禮拜寫咗一半，今個禮拜補埋另外一半——平價係手段，唔係目的。所以要睇嘅，唔係邊個發佈會開得大聲，而係邊個喺底下收錢。

一、美國開源賬單，89% 流咗去中國

Menlo Ventures 出咗份報告：美國企業喺開源模型上嘅支出，有 89% 付咗畀中國模型——Kimi 佔 61%、DeepSeek 佔 15%、GLM 佔 11%，西方陣營最高嘅 Llama 只有個位數。最值得睇嘅唔係 89% 呢個數，而係個排序：頭三位全部係中國公司，加埋已經食咗大部分。呢份報告係用採購數據砌出嚟，唔係用問卷砌出嚟；付錢嘅人，唔會同自己嘅成本講政治。

我判斷呢個先至係真正嘅投票。政客喺國會山講「中國 AI 威脅」，企業 CFO 喺採購單上簽名嗰陣，揀嘅係邊個平、邊個快、邊個開源得夠徹底。嘴同手，行嘅係兩套邏輯。票係用錢投嘅，唔係用演講投嘅。企業唔會為咗立場去買貴嘢，尤其係同一個任務，一邊收費、一邊免費。

呢個反差把美國本土嘅開源生態推向一邊：唔係滅亡，而係收窄——大模型嘅開源底座，慢慢變成中國公司嘅默認選項，美國公司愈嚟愈似喺人哋打嘅地基上面起樓。收窄難睇得過滅亡，因為枱面仲有模型、仲有下載量，只係下面嗰層唔再係自己嘅。

來源：36氪《美國企業的開源賬單，89% 給了中國模型》，2026-11-10｜https://m.36kr.com/p/4058059476440707

二、DeepSeek 開放全家桶做 API

DeepSeek Harness 上線唔夠三個月，日活已經 154 萬，日耗約 92 萬億 token，第三方插件 3.5 萬個。11 月 13 號晚，DeepSeek 再行多一步：把「全家桶應用」開成 API——聯網搜索、深度思考搜索、文件輸入、讀鏈接、安全校驗，OCR、ASR、TTS 仲可以單獨調用，連可導入歷史對話嘅前端 Demo 都開源埋。

我判斷呢一步嘅意義，大過出一個新模型。模型本身喺度貶值——上星期我寫過，模型越賣越平——真正值錢嘅係入口同習慣：當開發者用 DeepSeek 嘅搜索、識字、語音一條龍嚟砌產品，佢哋要搬走嘅成本會愈嚟愈高。搬走唔係技術問題，係要成個重寫一次。

把 App 開成 API，等於把用戶嘅使用習慣直接變成其他公司嘅地基。呢個係開放式嘅「綁定」：唔使鎖人，人哋自己唔捨得走。鎖匙喺人哋手，門係開住嘅，所以先至最難走。開發者最怕嘅唔係收費，係要重新學一套接口，再 debug 一次，搵一遍邊個位出事。

來源：36氪《DeepSeek Harness 上線不到三個月，日活 154 萬》，2026-11-08｜https://m.36kr.com/p/4057963796059017

三、美團把 1.6 萬億參數開源

美團呢個星期開放咗 LongCat-2.5 嘅權重：MoE 總參 1.6 萬億、MIT 許可，連 W4A4 量化權重同國產 NPU 部署方案都一齊畀。1.6 萬億呢個數一定上頭條，不過上頭條嗰個數，從來都唔係重點。呢個組合喺內地伺服器圈子嘅意思好直接：唔使等別人幫你適配，你自己就可以落地。

我判斷呢一步嘅關鍵，唔喺「大」，而喺「連部署方案一齊送」。MIT 許可等於冇任何限制；量化權重加國產 NPU 方案，等於話你知：攞去你張國產卡上面跑就得。開源唔止送模型，仲送「點樣喺自己塊地上面起樓」嘅圖則。送圖則嘅人，多數都想定埋樓嘅規矩。

當一家中國公司把 1.6 萬億參數連部署方案一齊開源，佢賣嘅唔係模型，係標準——邊個嘅開源模型成為默認底座，邊個就定下一代應用層嘅規矩。呢個唔靠情懷，靠嘅係下游冇得揀。標準呢樣嘢，從來唔係寫喺文件度，係寫喺別人嘅默認選項度。

來源：觀察者網《美團開放 1.6 萬億參數模型權重》，2026-11-13｜https://www.guancha.cn/CaiJing/2026_11_11_907882.shtml

四、百度把 Token 擺上櫃檯

百度世界開幕，實貨係大模型交易平台「詞元所」，官方稱日均 Token 破 7000 億；同場發咗天池 1024 卡超節點，崑崙芯 M100 已經上市。場面係發新品，實際係把「Token」由一個技術單位，改成一個價簽。Token 本來只係賬單上面一行數字，依家變成一塊可以報價嘅招牌。

我判斷呢個動作，同開源係同一件事嘅另一面：開源把「底座」送出去，詞元所把「計價單位」握喺手。Token 一旦變成可以喺平台上比較、交易嘅商品，邊個定標準、邊個就收租。收租穩定過賣貨，因為租係跟住用量走。平台一旦做咗定價嗰一方，後面嘅模型叻唔叻，都要跟佢把尺。

中國呢邊嘅打法愈嚟愈清楚：唔同美國嗰邊鬥「邊個模型最叻」，而係把模型嘅地基、部署、計價三樣嘢全部攞埋自己手。你喺上面起幾高嘅樓，地基都係人哋嘅。樓可以拆，地基拆唔走。所以我唔太關心下一個模型跑分幾多，我關心下一個默認選項係邊個。

來源：觀點新媒體《百度把 Token 擺上櫃檯》，2026-11-13｜https://www.sohu.com/a/1100671177_655634

五、鄭哥點評

呢個星期嘅開源攻勢，同上一篇我寫嘅「模型越賣越平」係同一件事嘅兩面：先把模型打成白菜價，再靠開放搶標準。美國喊「威脅」，喊嘅其實唔係模型，係地基——當全球開源項目嘅默認底座換成中國模型，上面每一層應用、每一家初創，都被迫建喺人哋打嘅地基上。呢個先係「威脅」兩個字真正嘅重量。

落到自己：本週同脣釉半成品出海無直接關係。但同一句教訓係通用嘅——底盤係邊個嘅，定價權就係邊個嘅。模型層如此，脣釉出海亦如此：料體同包材嘅錢被邊個賺走，唔係睇邊個做得靚，係睇邊個揸住標準同渠道。我哋做代工做咗咁耐，呢個道理一早寫喺賬簿度。

\[Zheng Ge's Weekly Take\] Column: Zheng Ge's weekly review | Date: 2026-11-16

This week I kept seeing the same thread in the morning briefs: the United States calls Chinese AI a top threat, yet pays eight or nine dollars out of every ten of its open-source model spending to Chinese companies. In the same week, DeepSeek opened its entire app as an API, Meituan handed out the weights of a 1.6-trillion-parameter model, and Baidu put tokens on a counter. Three things side by side do not look like coincidence; they look like one playbook landing in three directions. Add the three together and you get my judgment for this week: the fight has moved from "whose model is smarter" to "who holds the base layer and the unit of account". The cheaper models get, the harder the fight over the foundation. I wrote half of that sentence last week; this week I owe the other half — cheap is the method, not the goal. So what matters is not whose launch event is loudest, but who is collecting underneath.

89% of America's open-source bills flow to China

Menlo Ventures put out a report: 89% of US enterprises' open-source model spending goes to Chinese models — Kimi takes 61%, DeepSeek 15%, GLM 11%, while Llama, the West's top name, sits in single digits. The number worth staring at is not the 89% but the ranking: the top three are all Chinese, and together they already eat most of the market. The report is built from procurement data, not from questionnaires; people who pay do not play politics with their own costs.

I judge this is the real vote. Politicians on the Hill call Chinese AI a "threat"; the CFO signing the purchase order picks whoever is cheaper, faster and more thoroughly open. The mouth and the hand run on two different logics. Votes are cast with money, not with speeches. No enterprise buys the expensive option for the sake of a stance — especially when, for the same task, one side charges and the other is free.

This contrast pushes America's homegrown open-source ecosystem one way: not extinction, but narrowing — the open-source base layer quietly becomes a Chinese default, and American companies increasingly build on someone else's foundation. Narrowing is harder to see than extinction, because the table still holds models and still holds download counts; it is only the layer underneath that is no longer theirs.

Source: 36氪《美國企業的開源賬單，89% 給了中國模型》，2026-11-10 | https://m.36kr.com/p/4058059476440707

DeepSeek opens its whole app as an API

Less than three months after launch, DeepSeek Harness hit 1.54 million daily active users, roughly 92 trillion tokens of daily consumption, and 35,000 third-party plugins. On the evening of November 13, DeepSeek went one step further: it opened its "full-suite apps" as APIs — web search, deep-thought search, file input, link reading and safety checks, with OCR, ASR and TTS callable separately, and it even open-sourced a front-end demo that can import chat history.

I judge this move matters more than releasing a new model. Models are depreciating — last week I wrote that models keep getting cheaper — what is really worth money is the entry point and the habit: once developers build products on DeepSeek's search, OCR and voice in one go, the cost of moving away keeps rising. Moving away is not a technical problem; it means rewriting the whole thing once.

Turning the app into an API is turning users' habits into other companies' foundation. This is an open-ended "lock-in": nobody forces you to stay, you just do not want to leave. The key sits in someone else's hand and the door is left open — which is exactly why leaving is hardest. What developers dread is not the bill; it is learning a new set of interfaces, debugging it all over again, and hunting down which call broke.

Source: 36氪《DeepSeek Harness 上線不到三個月，日活 154 萬》，2026-11-08 | https://m.36kr.com/p/4057963796059017

Meituan open-sources 1.6 trillion parameters

This week Meituan opened the weights of LongCat-2.5: a 1.6-trillion-parameter MoE under an MIT license, bundled with W4A4 quantized weights and a domestic NPU deployment plan. The 1.6 trillion will make headlines for sure, but the headline number has never been the point. In mainland server circles that bundle says one thing plainly: you do not wait for someone else to port it, you can ship it yourself.

I judge the key is not the size but the fact that the deployment plan comes with it. An MIT license means zero restrictions; quantized weights plus a domestic-NPU plan means: take it and run it on your Chinese-made card, right now. Open-sourcing no longer means just giving away a model — it means giving away the blueprint for building on your own land. Whoever hands out blueprints usually wants to set the building code too.

When a Chinese company open-sources 1.6 trillion parameters together with the deployment recipe, it is not selling a model, it is selling a standard — whoever's open model becomes the default base layer gets to set the rules for the next generation of applications. That does not run on sentiment; it runs on the downstream having no other choice. A standard is never written in a document; it is written into other people's default options.

Source: 觀察者網《美團開放 1.6 萬億參數模型權重》，2026-11-13 | https://www.guancha.cn/CaiJing/2026_11_11_907882.shtml

Baidu puts tokens on the counter

Baidu World opened, and the real product was "Token Exchange", a marketplace for large-model tokens, with officials saying daily tokens topped 700 billion; the same event saw a 1024-card Tianchi super-node, and the Kunlunxin M100 is already on sale. The staging was a product launch; what happened was turning "token" from a technical unit into a price tag. A token used to be one line on an invoice; now it is a signboard you can quote a price on.

I judge this is the other side of the same coin as open-sourcing: open source hands out the base layer, the token exchange holds the unit of account. Once tokens become a commodity you can compare and trade on a platform, whoever sets the standard collects the rent. Rent is steadier than selling goods, because rent follows usage. Once a platform becomes the side that sets the price, every model behind it — smart or not — is measured with its ruler.

China's playbook is getting clearer: instead of fighting the US over "whose model is smartest", it takes the model's foundation, deployment and pricing all into its own hands. However tall you build on top, the foundation is someone else's. You can demolish a building; you cannot demolish the ground. So I care less about the next model's benchmark score than about who becomes the next default.

Source: 觀點新媒體《百度把 Token 擺上櫃檯》，2026-11-13 | https://www.sohu.com/a/1100671177_655634

5. Zheng Ge's Take

This week's open-source offensive is the other side of the coin from last week's "models keep getting cheaper": first turn models into a commodity, then grab the standard by opening up. When America says "threat", it doesn't mean the model — it means the foundation: once the default base layer of global open source becomes Chinese, every app and every startup above it is forced to build on someone else's ground. That is the real weight of the word "threat".

For ourselves: this week has no direct link to lip-gloss semi-finished exports. But the same lesson holds — whoever owns the base layer sets the price. True for models, and true for lip-gloss exports too: who captures the margin on formulas and packaging isn't decided by who makes them prettiest, but by who holds the standards and the channels. After all these years in OEM, that lesson has long been written in our ledger.
