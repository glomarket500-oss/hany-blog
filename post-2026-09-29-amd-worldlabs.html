AMD 550億收購李飛飛World Labs：AI軍備競賽進入新階段？AMD's $8.2B World Labs Deal — AI Arms Race Enters New Phase

2026年9月29日 21:24:04Sep 29, 2026 21:24:04 AI點評AI Review AMDAMD 李飛飛Fei-Fei Li
【鄭哥點評】【ZHENG GE REVIEW】

琴晚收到老友阿昌的WhatsApp，得閒瞄一眼新聞——AMD以82億美元收購李飛飛創辦的World Labs。鄭哥我做外銷26年，見慣併購，但係今次呢單，認真要寫一篇。

李飛飛係乜嘢人？計算機視覺領域的祖師奶奶，ImageNet創始人，曾經喺Google Cloud同Stanford兩邊走，而家轉頭做AI研究。82億美元——550億元人民幣——並購一個實驗室，呢個數字擺喺度，大家知唔知係乜嘢概念？

AMD歷史上第二大收購，僅次於2022年500億美元收購賽靈思（Xilinx）。而家你話我知，一間AI模型實驗室，點解值82億？

第一幕：華爾街看到的數字——82億美元背後

AMD周一（9月28日）喺官網發稿，全股票交易，預計年底完成。蘇姿豐親自出嚟解畫：「為下一代AI構建計算平台，需要深入理解模型如何演進。李飛飛同World Labs團隊帶嚟的研究領導力同模型專長，正好係我哋缺的嘢。」

李飛飛方面，佢的回應同樣直接：「加入AMD將為我哋團隊提供資源同工程深度，以加速研究，並幫助定義AI新時代所需的基礎設施。」

鄭哥我讀到呢度，馬上想起2017年我喺深圳見過的一位晶片廠老闆。佢當時話：「我哋中國人做半導體，永遠係落後食塵。但係我哋有市場，有人，有應用場景。」十年過去，今日的局面——AMD用550億買一個模型實驗室——完全印證了佢呢句說話：硬件廠商為咗軟件能力，願意付出幾何級數的溢價。

第二幕：阿昌的問題——李飛飛團隊究竟有幾值錢？

阿昌係我喺深圳認識多年的AI工程師，專做晶片設計自動化。佢WhatsApp問我：「鄭哥，World Labs究竟做啲乜？82億，值唔值？」

我答佢：你自己上網查。

佢查到的資料：2024年9月，李飛飛宣佈World Labs融資2.3億美元，估值約10億美元。2026年2月，新一輪融資10億美元——估值冇公佈，但係投資者名單有a16z、Radical Ventures，仲有NVIDIA、AMD、Autodesk。

投資者名單係解讀關鍵：NVIDIA、AMD呢啲硬件廠商，點解要投資一間AI模型公司？

答案好簡單：AI模型決定硬件需求。如果唔控制上游模型的走向，硬件廠商就好似揼住大量資金起工廠，但係唔知客人究竟想要乜嘢規格。

阿昌話：「即係話，蘇姿豐買的唔係李飛飛呢個人，係買佢對AI模型走向的判斷力？」

我話：「差唔多咁解。」

第三幕：數據中心——中國巨頭唔輸

華爾街的收購之餘，我哋中國呢邊亦冇停。

阿里雲喺雲棲大會宣佈：目標到2032年，全球數據中心規模超過20GW。20GW——呢個數字意味住乜嘢？意味住相當於大約20個標準燃煤電廠的發電容量，全部用嚟跑AI計算。

吳泳銘喺同一個場合話：「未來機器思考總量將達到人類的1000倍以上。」佢把AI模型、AI晶片同AI雲定義為「機器智能時代的三大基石」。

鄭哥我聽到呢句說話，第一反應係：好大膽。但係再諗深一層，淘寶當年話「讓天下沒有難做生意」嗰陣，大家一樣話佢吹牛皮。今日點？

第四幕：鄭哥點評——AI軍備競賽，緊係鬥乜？

總結今日所見，我畫一條線俾阿昌：

美國：AMD收購World Labs → 硬件廠商控制上游模型 → 綁定軟件棧

中國：阿里20GW數據中心 → 吳泳銘「機器思考1000倍」 → 底層建設先行

兩條路，邊條行得通？

鄭哥我的判斷：短期，美國模式見效快——蘇姿豐買的是人才，李飛飛團隊馬上可以用喺AMD的GPU上面。長期，中国模式後勁強——20GW規模，唔係靠併購可以短期堆出嚟，需要時間沉澱。

但係最關鍵的變量，唔喺硬件，唔喺軟件，喺應用場景——邊個能夠將AI變現，邊個就係最後贏家。

AMD 82億美元收購World Labs，唔係一間公司的事，而係整個AI產業的併購時代正式開始的信號。

Last night I received a WhatsApp from my old friend Ah-Chang, took a quick look at the news — AMD acquiring World Labs founded by Fei-Fei Li for $8.2 billion. After 26 years in export, I've seen plenty of M&A deals, but this one, I had to write about.

Who is Fei-Fei Li? The godmother of computer vision, founder of ImageNet, someone who's moved between Google Cloud and Stanford, now turning to AI research. $8.2 billion — 55 billion RMB — for an AI lab. Do people understand what this number means?

AMD's second-largest acquisition in history, only behind the 2022 $50B Xilinx deal. Now tell me, why is an AI model lab worth $8.2B?

Act 1: The Numbers on Wall Street — What's Behind the $8.2B

AMD issued its press release on Monday (September 28) on its official website, all-stock transaction, expected to close by year-end. Lisa Su personally came out to explain: "Building compute platforms for the next generation of AI requires deep understanding of how models evolve. What Fei-Fei Li and the World Labs team bring in research leadership and model expertise is exactly what we need."

Fei-Fei Li's response was equally direct: "Joining AMD will give our team resources and engineering depth to accelerate research and help define the infrastructure needed for the AI era."

When I read this, I immediately thought of a chip factory owner I met in Shenzhen in 2017. He told me: "Chinese people doing semiconductors, we're always playing catch-up. But we have the market, the people, the application scenarios." Ten years later, today's situation — AMD spending 55 billion buying an AI model lab — fully confirms what he said: hardware companies are willing to pay geometric-level premiums for software capabilities.

Act 2: Ah-Chang's Question — How Much Is Fei-Fei Li's Team Really Worth?

Ah-Chang is an AI engineer I've known for years in Shenzhen, specializing in chip design automation. He WhatsApp'd me: "Zheng Ge, what does World Labs actually do? Is $8.2B worth it?"

I told him: look it up yourself.

What he found: September 2024, Fei-Fei Li announced World Labs raised $230M at about $1B valuation. February 2026, a new round of $1B raised — valuation not disclosed, but the investor list included a16z, Radical Ventures, plus NVIDIA, AMD, and Autodesk.

The investor list is the key: why would hardware vendors like NVIDIA and AMD invest in an AI model company?

The answer is simple: AI models determine hardware requirements. If you don't control the direction of upstream models, hardware vendors are like dumping huge capital to build factories without knowing what specs the customers actually want.

Ah-Chang said: "So Lisa Su isn't buying Fei-Fei Li the person, she's buying her judgment on where AI models are going?"

I said: "Something like that."

Act 3: Data Centers — Chinese Giants Aren't Losing

While Wall Street was doing its acquisition, China didn't stand still either.

Alibaba Cloud announced at the Yunqi Conference: target to exceed 20GW of global data center capacity by 2032. 20GW — what does this number mean? It means equivalent to about 20 standard coal-fired power plants' generating capacity, all used for AI computing.

Wu Yongming at the same event said: "The total machine thinking will reach over 1000 times that of humans in the future." He defined AI models, AI chips, and AI cloud as "the three cornerstones of the machine intelligence era."

When I heard this, my first reaction was: bold. But thinking deeper, when Taobao back then said "making it easy to do business anywhere," everyone said they were吹牛皮. How about today?

Act 4: Zheng Ge's Take — What Are We Actually Fighting For in the AI Arms Race?

Summarizing what I saw today, I drew a line for Ah-Chang:

US: AMD acquires World Labs -> hardware vendor controls upstream model -> locks in software stack

China: Alibaba 20GW data center -> Wu Yongming's "1000x machine thinking" -> infrastructure first

Two paths, which one works?

My judgment: short-term, the US model takes effect faster — Lisa Su is buying talent, Fei-Fei Li's team can immediately work on AMD's GPUs. Long-term, the China model has stronger stamina — 20GW scale can't be built overnight through acquisitions, it requires time to accumulate.

But the most critical variable isn't hardware, isn't software, it's application scenarios — whoever can monetize AI is the final winner.

AMD's $8.2B acquisition of World Labs isn't one company's business — it's the signal that the M&A era of the entire AI industry has officially begun.
