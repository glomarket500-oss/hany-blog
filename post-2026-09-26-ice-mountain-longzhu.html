冰岛古树龙珠一粒一泡，喝的是方便，还是古树那口甜Ice Mountain Ancient-Tree Dragon Pearls: One Ball, One Steep — Convenience or Ancient-Tree Sweetness?

2026年9月26日 18:56:00Sep 26, 2026 18:56:00 茶葉點評Tea Review 冰岛古树Ice Mountain 普洱龙珠Puerh Dragon Ball
【鄭哥點評】【ZHENG GE REVIEW】

昨晚中山做茶的老友老何，發來一組圖，木禮盒上面刻住私家茶園，下面一行拼音SIJIACHAYUAN，盒蓋打開係一棵大樹的相，寫住珍稀古樹茶。木盤上幾粒黑亮的龍珠，旁邊一壺茶湯，一杯葉底。老何話，鄭哥，冰岛古樹龍珠，一斤裝，你幫我品下，值唔值。

我望住手機睇咗好耐。包裝幾靚，山野古韻，歲月沉香，冰糖甜幾個字都齊。但我做外貿咁多年，包裝越靚，越要沉住氣，睇茶唔睇盒。

第一幕：盒子同龍珠，先講方便呢件事

先講呢個盒。淺色木盒，金屬扣，上面刻梯田同茶山線畫，幾有心思。入面仲有一磚用紙包住的茶磚，盒蓋內襯就係嗰張古樹相。做禮品，拎得出手。

再講龍珠。一粒一泡，獨立包裝，唔使撬茶，唔使秤。特寫嗰粒，條索緊壓，深褐帶金芽，喺自封袋上面，幾精神。呢個形態係為咗咩，為咗方便。辦公室、差旅、請朋友，一粒搞掂。同而家茶圈講的日常消費轉向啱啱對上，9月23日南方企業新聞網嗰篇講得好白，高端禮品茶收縮，300到800蚊一公斤的中端品質茶成為主流，仲有單叢紅碎茶、超微茶粉、速溶茶，全部都係將夏秋茶變方便。龍珠都係同一條路，將古樹料變成隨手可泡。

私家茶園主阿野同我講過，龍珠最難係壓得勻，太鬆散味，太緊化唔開。呢批睇相，壓得算緊實，後面睇湯就知有冇壓死。

第二幕：公道杯入面的湯，琥珀色會講嘢

老何發來兩張湯。一張係玻璃公道杯，底上淺淺一層，橙黃透亮，杯壁仲有霧氣。一張係手拎住公道杯，茶湯金黃帶橙，幾乾淨。

鄭哥點評湯，就睇三樣。第一係乾淨度，呢兩杯都透，無渾。第二係顏色，橙黃金黃，係生普轉咗幾年應有的色，未去到深紅，估計年份唔算老。包裝上寫住很大的樹，古樹料做的，珍稀古樹料，呢啲係宣傳語，唔好當年份。係咪06年的料，一張圖講唔清，要喝先知。

第三係濃度。公道杯底嗰層睇落唔算厚，唔係濃強型。冰岛古樹本來就行冰糖甜、苦澀低、化得快、生津猛的路線，唔係靠濃去壓人。呢個湯色，同呢個方向吻合。係咪真冰岛老寨，要入口先有數，但至少個湯無露怯。

第三幕：葉底嗰杯，係誠實的

我最鍾意睇嗰張葉底。白色小杯入面，葉片泡開，深褐帶橄欖綠，肥厚，扭得開。耐泡度一般睇呢度，葉片厚，十泡八泡應該頂得住。正宗冰岛老寨有個講法，葉底肥厚，耐泡十到十二泡。呢杯葉底，算交到功課。

老何話，鄭哥，葉底咁靚，係咪真古樹。我話，葉底靚只證明料唔差，唔證明係邊個山頭。而家市面號稱冰岛的多如牛毛，外山料小樹茶冒充，普通人好難分。9月16日東方印象嗰篇甄選指南講得直接，真正核心產區純料少之又少，選茶首先係選廠，選源頭供應鏈同良心。仲有9月21日中華普洱茶網嗰單，冰岛古樹茶價十年漲近200倍，古樹單株一公斤四到六萬。咁貴的料，一斤龍珠賣幾多錢先合理，心裡要有杆秤。太平，一定唔係老寨純料。太貴，就要問有冇源頭背書。

第四幕：存量時代，茶同建材一個道理

呢幾日我成日諗起陳老闆嗰句很不好。建材靠補貨，裝一次管十年。茶呢，班章為王，冰岛為后，炒作退潮之後，都係返到日常飲用。9月23日嗰篇行業分析話，金融茶泡沫出清，茶行業返到以茶為本，大眾日常消費、深加工、高附加值出口撐住下一階段。

龍珠呢個形態，啱啱就係日常飲用。唔使撬，唔使諗，一粒一泡，辦公室都飲得。雅安藏茶都做成瓶裝即飲，一年200萬瓶，茶磚變成飲料。大家都在做同一件事，將傳統變方便，將禮品變日常。

老何問我，鄭哥，你一句，買唔買得。我話三句。第一，盒靚料未必差，但買茶唔好為盒畀錢，為湯畀錢。第二，龍珠圖方便，要試一粒，睇化不化得開，睇甜不甜，睇回甘長唔長，睇十泡之後仲有冇嘢。第三，要問源頭，邊個寨，哪年春茶，邊個初制所，答得出的先係自己人，答唔出的幾靚都係故事。

一句話總結

龍珠一粒，方便係真方便，古樹係咪真古樹，要湯同葉底講了算，仲要源頭撐得起。

Last night, my old friend Lao He — a tea maker in Zhongshan — sent me a set of photos. A light wooden gift box, carved with the words 私家茶園 and a photo of a giant ancient tree inside the lid. On a wooden tray: several glossy dark Puerh dragon balls, a pot of tea soup, and a cup of infused leaves. "Hany, ice mountain ancient-tree dragon pearls, one jin. Tell me, are they worth it?"

I stared at the phone for a while. The packaging was beautiful — mountain wild charm, aged fragrance, "ice sugar sweetness" — all the right words. But after 26 years in trade, I know: the fancier the box, the more you need to stay calm. Judge the tea, not the packaging.

First Act: The Box and the Pearl — Let's Talk Convenience First

First, the box. Light-colored wood, metal clasps, etched with terraced fields and tea mountain lines. Inside, a paper-wrapped tea cake, and the lid liner is that ancient-tree photo. A proper gift box.

Then the dragon ball. One ball, one steep. No need to pry, no need to weigh. The close-up shows tightly rolled, dark brown with golden buds, sitting on a resealable bag — immaculately presented. This shape exists for one reason: convenience. Office, travel, entertaining guests — one ball does the job. This matches exactly what the tea world now calls the shift to daily consumption. A September 23 Southern Enterprise News article put it plainly: premium gift tea is contracting, mid-range quality tea at 300–800 yuan per kg is becoming the mainstream. Single-cultivar red broken tea, ultra-micro tea powder, instant tea — all turning summer-autumn leaves into convenience. Dragon pearls are the same path, transforming ancient-tree material into something you can steep anywhere.

Wild Garden's owner Ayye once told me: the hardest part of dragon pearls is even compression. Too loose and the flavor disperses; too tight and it won't open. From these photos, the compression looks tight but not excessive. The tea soup will tell if it's been pressed dead.

Second Act: What the Fairness Cup Says

Lao He sent two soup photos. One: a glass fairness cup with a shallow golden-amber layer, bright and transparent, condensation on the glass walls. The other: a hand holding the fairness cup, the tea soup golden-orange, spotlessly clean.

Hany's tea checklist: three things. First, cleanliness — both cups pass, no murkiness. Second, color — golden-orange is what aged raw Puerh should look like, nowhere near deep red, suggesting the year isn't old. The box says "big ancient tree," "rare ancient-tree material" — that's marketing. Don't confuse it with age. Whether it's a 2006 material, one photo can't confirm. Only drinking tells. Third, body. The thin layer at the bottom isn't thick — not a strong-intensity tea. Ice mountain ancient tree naturally walks the path of ice-sugar sweetness, low astringency, fast hua, and vigorous salivation — not relying on density to impress. The soup color is consistent with this profile. Whether it's the real old Ice Mountain village, only the sip knows. But at least the soup doesn't embarrass itself.

Third Act: The Leaf Cup Never Lies

My favorite photo is the leaf base. In a small white cup, the leaves unfurl — dark brown with olive green, thick and fleshy, able to be spread open. Steeping endurance shows here: thick leaves should handle eight to ten steeps. Authentic Ice Mountain old village saying: thick leaf base, ten to twelve steeps. This cup delivers on that front.

Lao He asks: "Hany, the leaf base is so nice, is it really ancient tree?" I say: a beautiful leaf base only proves the material isn't bad; it doesn't prove which mountain it's from. The market is flooded with self-proclaimed "ice mountain" products, with外山料 and small-tree tea masquerading as the real thing. An September 16 East Impression selection guide was direct: genuine core-area single-cultivar is extremely rare. Choosing tea starts with choosing the factory, the traceable supply chain, and integrity. And from the September 21 Chinese Puerh Tea Net report: ice mountain ancient-tree tea price has risen nearly 200 times in a decade, with ancient single-tree at 40,000–60,000 yuan per kg. At that price, what is a reasonable price for a jin of dragon pearls? There must be a scale in your heart. Too cheap, it's definitely not old-village single-cultivar. Too expensive, ask whether there's source backing.

Fourth Act: The Stock Era — Tea Follows the Same Logic as Building Materials

These past few days I keep thinking about Hardware Shop Chen's words: "pretty bad." Building materials survives on refills; one renovation holds for a decade. Tea? Bangchang is king, Ice Mountain is queen. Once the speculation tide recedes, both return to daily drinking. A September 23 industry analysis piece noted: financial tea bubble clearing, the tea industry returning to tea as the foundation, with mass daily consumption, deep processing, and high-value export supporting the next phase.

The dragon pearl format fits daily consumption perfectly. No prying, no thinking, one ball one steep — drinkable even in the office. Ya'an Tibetan tea has gone bottled and ready-to-drink, 2 million bottles a year. Tea bricks turned into beverages. Everyone is doing the same thing: making tradition convenient, turning gifts into daily rituals.

Lao He asks: "Hany, in one sentence — worth buying?" Three sentences. One: beautiful packaging doesn't mean bad material, but don't pay for the box; pay for the soup. Two: for the convenience of dragon pearls, try one ball — see if it opens, if it's sweet, if the回甘 lingers, if there's anything left after ten steeps. Three: ask for the source. Which village, which spring tea, which primary processing workshop. Whoever can answer is one of us. Whoever can't — however beautiful the story, it's just a story.

One-Line Summary

One dragon pearl, convenience is genuinely convenient — whether the ancient tree is truly ancient depends on the soup and the leaf base, plus a source that can back it up.
