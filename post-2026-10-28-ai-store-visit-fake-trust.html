《鄭哥點評：AI探店不到10元一條，800萬月流水的生意，賣的是什麼？》Zheng Ge's Take: AI Store-Visit Videos at Under 10 Yuan a Clip, an 8-Million-Yuan-a-Month Business — What Is It Really Selling?

2026年10月28日Oct 28, 2026 AI探店AI store visits 虛假宣傳False advertising
【鄭哥點評】欄目：鄭哥博客點評｜日期：2026-10-28｜關鍵詞：AI探店、探店不到店、月流水800萬、虛假宣傳、AI標識辦法

原文核心觀點

事件：證券時報10月26日頭版報道，AI視頻生成技術成熟後，「探店」不再需要「到店」。

關鍵數字（記者實測）：

  • 閒魚上AI探店教程：0.18元起，普遍1元左右
  • 工具積分：1積分=1元，最低充值20元
  • 年度會員：299元/年
  • 一條12秒480P的探店視頻：9.6積分=9.6元
  • 頭部批量製作公司月流水：800萬元
  • 代運營報價：奶茶小吃300-500元/條，火鍋燒烤美容600-800元/條
  • 單條傳統探店成本：數千元的量級

流程：上傳門店環境+商品特寫+模特圖片 → 補充門店信息 → 腳本自動生成 → 選模型/比例/時長/分辨率 → 一鍵成片。全程不用真人出鏡、不用寫文案、不用到店實拍。

風險：

北大匯豐商學院副教授葉韋明：「探店的核心價值在於『體驗的真實性』，AI數字人給出的評估和推薦從根本上違背了人類真實的消費需求。」

杭州中院今年6月已判：AI代寫「種草筆記」構成不正當競爭，被告停止侵權並賠償損失。

2025年9月1日《人工智能生成合成內容標識辦法》已施行——AI生成的文本、圖片、音頻、視頻必須在顯著位置添加用戶可明顯感知的標識。但短視頻平台上，「主動標識的執行率不高」。

廣東連越（東莞）律師事務所律師黃泰瑜：博主壓根沒去過、直接用商家素材讓AI生成完整體驗的，可能觸犯《刑法》第222條的虛假廣告罪；運用AI換臉、克隆知名人物聲音形象的，還涉及侵犯肖像權、聲音權。

規則紅線：AI工具應服務於文案潤色、剪輯製作等技術環節，不能取代真人到店的體驗真實性。

鄭哥點評：這篇報道沒算清的賬

報道本身很紮實，記者實測、律師採訪、學者意見、司法判例、監管規則齊全。但它把一件事講成了「技術顛覆成本」，我卻覺得它其實是「信任工業化」——兩碼事，賬要分開算。

一、成本賬：9.6元和600元之間，差的根本不是算力

記者實測9.6元一條，代運營報價600-800元。中間的500-700元差價，不是算力差價——算力就值那9.6元。

那商家為什麼願意付600-800？

買的是分發和信任背書。

  • 分發：代運營有矩陣號、有達人分發渠道、有投流經驗，視頻發出去了能進信息流、能被算法推、能帶團購轉化
  • 信任：以「達人到店測評」的形態出現，用戶看到的是「真人測評」，不是「商家自嗨」

AI視頻本身值9.6元，包裝成「達人測評」這個殼才值600元。

這就是問題所在——如果代運營純AI生成，毛利接近100%；而商家自己DIY幾乎零成本。商家為什麼還要付600？因為他要的不是視頻，是「達人號」這個身份。AI把最不值錢的那個東西（視頻本身）做便宜了，反而把最值錢的那個（達人的信任溢價）暴露成了明碼標價的包裝費。

這個賬被報道一筆帶過了。

二、市場賬：800萬月流水，意味着什麼

800萬/月，按火鍋檔均價600-800元算，一個月要交付10000-13000條視頻。

一天300-400條。

這數字什麼概念？比全國任何一家傳統探店工作室的產能都高幾個數量級——傳統博主一條視頻要到店、拍攝、剪輯、投流，一天最多出1條，快一點也是2條。

800萬/月不是「批量製作」的量級，這是「視頻工廠」的量級。

工廠邏輯一進來，「探店」這個業態就被工業邏輯改寫了：

  • 稀缺性沒了——真人到店體驗本來是最稀缺、最貴的資產，現在9.6元批量生產
  • 門檻倒掛——做一條真探店要成本數千，做一條AI假探店9.6元。理性的商家當然選後者
  • 劣幣驅逐——真探店視頻的曝光被AI假探店淹沒，用戶越來越分不清，越來越不信

這就是葉韋明說的「流量套利和虛假繁榮」——不是AI讓探店變得更好，是AI讓造假變得比造假前的造假更廉價。

三、法律賬：紅線已經畫，但執法還在灰區

報道里兩條判例和一部法規，都是真的、都有效：

  • 杭州中院（2026-06）：AI代寫種草筆記構成不正當競爭，判停止侵權+賠償。這是全國首例，信號很明確——「沒到店也生成測評」這個行為本身就已經違法，不用等到用戶告商家
  • 《人工智能生成合成內容標識辦法》（2025-09-01施行）：AI生成內容必須在顯著位置加標識
  • 《刑法》第222條 虛假廣告罪：情節嚴重可入刑

但報道自己也承認：「平台雖然有要求發佈者主動標識，也上線了輔助功能，但主動標識的執行率不高。」

一句話：法律有了、判例有了、監管辦法有了，執行還在原地。

為什麼執行難？報道里其實點了：

  • 代運營公司可能轉了好幾手，找不到責任主體
  • 普通人維權成本高，「算下來可能划不來」
  • 純文字內容規避關鍵詞，平台很難判定是否AI生成

這是所有新興內容業態的通用死結——規則跑在監管前面，監管跑在執法前面，執法跑在用戶認知前面。

我的判斷：未來6-12個月會有第二例、第三例判例出來，同時會有一波集中下架。 現在正是「紅線已畫但還沒被真正踩痛」的窗口期。

四、生意賬：真機會在哪

報道最後一段其實說了關鍵——「AI工具並非不能用，但其準則應是服務於文案潤色、剪輯製作等技術環節，不能取代真人到店的體驗真實性。」

這句話把真機會和假機會分清了。

真機會：

  1. 給真到店做剪輯潤色——你真人到店拍完，AI幫你剪輯、調色、加字幕、剪多版本。成本從3000元降到300元，還是「真到店」，還值錢
  2. 給本地商家做「AI輔助版探店」——真人到店是核心，AI做的是效率（多機位一鍵合成、腳本快速成稿、多平台分發適配）
  3. 做「AI素材真偽鑑別」服務——平台和企業端都會需要，這是防守型生意，法律一硬需求立刻起來

假機會：

  1. 純AI虛構體驗批量生產——杭州判例已經判了，就是給自己挖坑
  2. 教人做AI假探店——0.18元教程賣得便宜，但教人違法的鏈條風險在自己頭上
  3. 代運營純換皮AI——毛利100%是好東西，但一次被扒就全崩

一句話結論

9.6元買到的不是「探店視頻的工業化」，是「信任資產被明碼標價化」。視頻不值錢的那天，「到店」本身的價值反而被重新定義——因為它是唯一沒法用9.6元買到的東西。

做本地生活的商家要注意，流量套利的時代結束了，接下來是口碑硬碰硬。做AI工具和服務的要注意，監管的耐心是有底線的，杭州判例已經是發令槍。

資料來源：證券時報／21經濟網，2026年10月26日（原文經今日頭條轉載）；北大匯豐商學院副教授葉韋明；廣東連越（東莞）律師事務所律師黃泰瑜；北京輝諾律師事務所律師馬安然；杭州中院2026年6月全國首例涉AI代寫「種草筆記」不正當競爭案通報；《人工智能生成合成內容標識辦法》（2025年9月1日施行）；《刑法》第222條。

成本測算：AI探店單條真實成本 = 9.6元算力 + 會員分攤1.25元 ≈ 10.85元/條；對比傳統探店2000-5000元/條、代運營300-800元/條。

What the report actually says

What happened: on 26 October the Securities Times ran a front-page report. Now that AI video generation has matured, a "store visit" no longer requires going to the store.

The key numbers (measured by the reporter):

  • AI store-visit tutorials on Xianyu: from 0.18 yuan, typically around 1 yuan
  • Tool credits: 1 credit = 1 yuan, minimum top-up 20 yuan
  • Annual membership: 299 yuan a year
  • One 12-second 480P store-visit video: 9.6 credits = 9.6 yuan
  • Monthly revenue of a leading bulk-production company: 8 million yuan
  • Agency quotes: milk-tea and snack shops 300-500 yuan a video; hotpot, barbecue and beauty 600-800 yuan
  • Cost of one traditional store visit: in the thousands of yuan

The workflow: upload the shop interior + product close-ups + model photos → add the shop's information → the script writes itself → choose model / aspect ratio / length / resolution → one click and the video is finished. No real person on camera, no copy to write, no visit to film.

The risks:

Ye Weiming, associate professor at Peking University HSBC Business School: "The core value of a store visit is the authenticity of the experience. The assessments and recommendations of an AI digital human fundamentally run against real human consumption needs."

The Hangzhou Intermediate People's Court ruled in June this year: AI ghost-writing "seed-planting notes" constitutes unfair competition; the defendant stopped the infringement and paid damages.

The Measures for Labelling AI-Generated Synthetic Content took effect on 1 September 2025 — text, images, audio and video generated by AI must carry, in a prominent position, a label the user can clearly perceive. But on the short-video platforms, "the rate of voluntary labelling is not high".

Huang Taiyu, a lawyer at Guangdong Lianyue (Dongguan) Law Firm: where the blogger never went at all and simply fed a merchant's own footage to AI to generate a complete "experience", this may constitute the crime of false advertising under Article 222 of the Criminal Law; using AI face-swapping, or cloning a well-known person's voice and likeness, also engages portrait rights and voice rights.

The red line in the rules: AI tools should serve technical steps such as copy polishing and editing. They cannot replace the authenticity of a real person's visit.

Zheng Ge's take: the ledger this report never balanced

The report itself is solid — the reporter's own testing, interviews with lawyers, academic opinion, a court ruling, the regulatory rules: all of it is there. But it turns one thing into "technology disrupting cost", whereas I think it is actually "the industrialisation of trust" — two different things, and the two ledgers have to be kept apart.

1. The cost ledger: between 9.6 yuan and 600 yuan, the gap is not computing power

The reporter measured 9.6 yuan a video; agencies quote 600-800 yuan. The 500-700 yuan in between is not a computing-power premium — the computing power is worth that 9.6 yuan and nothing more.

So why will a merchant pay 600-800?

What they are buying is distribution and trust endorsement.

  • Distribution: an agency has matrix accounts, creator distribution channels and paid-traffic experience. Once the video is out it can enter the feed, be pushed by the algorithm, and drive group-buy conversions.
  • Trust: it arrives in the shape of "a creator's visit and review", so what the user sees is "a real person's review", not "the merchant praising himself".

The AI video itself is worth 9.6 yuan. It is worth 600 yuan only once it is packed in the shell of a "creator review".

And that is exactly the problem — if an agency generates everything with AI, gross margin approaches 100%; and a merchant doing it himself spends almost nothing. So why pay 600? Because what he wants is not the video but the identity of "a creator account". AI made the least valuable thing (the video itself) cheap, and in doing so exposed the most valuable thing (the creator's trust premium) as a packaging fee with a price tag on it.

The report skips over that ledger.

2. The market ledger: what 8 million yuan a month actually means

8 million yuan a month, at the hotpot tier's average of 600-800 yuan, means 10,000-13,000 videos delivered in a month.

300-400 a day.

What kind of number is that? It sits several orders of magnitude above the capacity of any traditional store-visit studio in the country — a traditional creator has to travel to the shop, shoot, edit and push, and can turn out one video a day at most; two on a fast day.

8 million yuan a month is not "bulk production" scale. It is "video factory" scale.

Once factory logic walks in, the whole trade of the "store visit" is rewritten by industrial logic:

  • Scarcity is gone — a real person's visit was the scarcest and most expensive asset of all; now it is mass-produced for 9.6 yuan.
  • The threshold is inverted — one real store visit costs thousands; one fake AI store visit costs 9.6 yuan. A rational merchant picks the latter, obviously.
  • Bad money drives out good — real store-visit videos are drowned out by fake AI ones; users can tell the difference less and less, and believe less and less.

This is what Ye Weiming calls "traffic arbitrage and false prosperity" — AI did not make store visits better; it made faking cheaper than the faking that came before it.

3. The legal ledger: the line is drawn, enforcement is still grey

The two rulings and the one regulation in the report are all real, and all in force:

  • Hangzhou Intermediate People's Court (2026-06): AI ghost-writing of seed-planting notes constitutes unfair competition; infringement stopped plus damages. It is the first case of its kind in the country and the signal is clear — "generating a review without ever visiting" is itself already unlawful; nobody has to wait for a user to sue the merchant.
  • The Measures for Labelling AI-Generated Synthetic Content (in force 2025-09-01): AI-generated content must carry a label in a prominent position.
  • Article 222 of the Criminal Law, false advertising: serious cases can carry a criminal sentence.

But the report itself admits: "Although the platforms require publishers to label voluntarily and have launched helper features, the rate of voluntary labelling is not high."

In one sentence: the law exists, the precedents exist, the regulation exists — enforcement has not moved.

Why is enforcement hard? The report actually points at it:

  • an agency may have been subcontracted several times over, so the responsible party cannot be found;
  • for an ordinary person the cost of defending a right is high — "when you do the sums it may not be worth it";
  • pure-text content dodges the keywords, so a platform can hardly judge whether it was AI-generated.

This is the common deadlock of every new content business — the rules run ahead of the regulator, the regulator runs ahead of enforcement, and enforcement runs ahead of what users understand.

My judgement: in the next 6-12 months a second and then a third precedent will come, and there will be a wave of takedowns at the same time. Right now is precisely the window in which "the line has been drawn but nobody has really been hurt by it yet".

4. The business ledger: where the real opening is

The last paragraph of the report actually says the key thing — "AI tools are not unusable, but the principle should be that they serve technical steps such as copy polishing and editing; they cannot replace the authenticity of a real person's visit."

That sentence separates the real opening from the fake one.

The real openings:

  1. Editing and polishing for real visits — you go and shoot in person, and AI edits, grades, subtitles and cuts it into several versions. Cost falls from 3,000 yuan to 300, it is still "a real visit", and it still has value.
  2. "AI-assisted store visits" for local merchants — the real visit is the core; AI supplies the efficiency (multi-camera assembly in one click, a fast script draft, distribution cut to fit each platform).
  3. "AI material authenticity checks" as a service — platforms and enterprises will both need it. It is a defensive business, and demand jumps the moment the law hardens.

The fake openings:

  1. Bulk production of purely AI-fabricated experiences — the Hangzhou ruling has already decided this one; it is digging your own hole.
  2. Teaching people to make fake AI store visits — the 0.18 yuan tutorial is cheap to sell, but the chain that teaches people to break the law carries its risk on your own head.
  3. Agencies that just re-skin AI output — 100% gross margin is a fine thing, but one expose and the whole thing collapses.

In one sentence

What 9.6 yuan buys is not "the industrialisation of the store-visit video". It is "trust as an asset, priced and labelled". On the day the video is worth nothing, the value of actually going is redefined — because it is the one thing that cannot be bought for 9.6 yuan.

Merchants in local life should note: the era of traffic arbitrage is over; what comes next is reputation against reputation. Those building AI tools and services should note: regulators' patience has a floor, and the Hangzhou ruling was already the starting gun.
