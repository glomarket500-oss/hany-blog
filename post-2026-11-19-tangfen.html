13蚊一碗牛肉粉，一日賣到200碗：小店嘅錢唔喺流量度Thirteen-Yuan Beef Noodles, 200 Bowls a Day: The Money Is Not in the Traffic

2026年11月19日 19:16:562026-11-19 19:16:56 小微生意Small business 深圳・南山Nanshan, Shenzhen
【鄭哥點評】欄目：鄭哥博客點評｜日期：2026-11-19

一條短視頻，把一間開喺職業學院旁邊、位置偏僻嘅湯粉店，由日賣 20 碗頂到 200 碗。我睇完第一個反應唔係「好勵志」，而係「13 蚊一碗，佢到底靠乜嘢活」。

一、先講事實：20 碗變 200 碗

深圳南山，一間開喺深圳職業技術學院附近、位置算偏僻嘅湯粉店，老闆係覃大叔一家人。11 月 12 日，一位在附近上班嘅博主隨手拍咗公司樓下呢間店，之後微博、抖音同城熱搜，超過 300 萬人關注。

結果係日銷量由 20 碗衝到 200 碗。打工人同學生專程趕嚟排隊，深職院學生都加入。紅燒牛肉粉 13 蚊一碗，其餘粉麵基本唔超過 20 蚊，曝光量 300 萬以上。

來源：鳳凰網｜https://feng.ifeng.com/c/8y6DgBicF86

二、13 蚊一碗，錢從邊度嚟

一碗 13 蚊嘅粉，扣埋食材、水電、人工，毛利本身唔厚。呢類店嘅活法係三樣嘢疊住：位置夠偏、租金夠低；出品夠快，一個灶頭一日出得夠多；客源固定——附近學生同打工仔，唔靠遊客。

所以流量嚟到，佢最直接嘅反應係「產能拉滿」，唔係「加價」。因為加價會趕走原本嗰班固定客，而固定客才係佢捱過淡季嘅底。

仲有一層：一碗粉嘅成本結構係「食材佔比高、人工固定、租金固定」。賣 20 碗嘅時候，租金同人工攤到每一碗身上極重；賣到 200 碗，同一份租金攤薄十倍，邊際成本急跌。這就是為什麼小店最怕「唔夠旺」，而唔係最怕「賺得少」——閒置嘅灶頭同閒置嘅舖位，係同一筆錢。

來源：鳳凰網｜https://feng.ifeng.com/c/8y6DgBicF86

三、為咩偏偏係呢類店爆紅

同類店喺深圳有幾萬間，為咩係佢爆？我睇係「反差」四個字。位置偏僻、門面唔靚、價錢平、老闆係一對老實夫妻——呢四樣湊埋一齊，同平台演算法想要嘅「真實感」正好對上。算法推嘅唔係最好食，係最似「我身邊嗰間」。

第二個條件係「可驗證」。13 蚊一碗、日賣 200 碗，呢兩個數觀眾一睇就明，唔需要專業知識，所以佢可以被覆述、被轉發、被同城推薦。相反，一間人均 300 蚊嘅餐廳就算好食十倍，都唔會有同樣嘅傳播力——因為大部分人冇代入感。

但這裡有個殘酷嘅推論：**爆紅係內容事件，唔係生意事件**。內容事件帶嚟嘅係一次脈衝，生意事件講嘅係月租、人工、出品穩定度。兩件事經常被混為一談，所以每次有店爆紅，都會有一批人衝去同類位置開店，然後半年後轉讓。

來源：香港商報｜https://www.hkcd.com/

三、鄭哥點評：呢門生意值不值得學

呢單新聞最值錢嘅唔係「一夜爆紅」，而係佢暴露咗小店嘅生死線：**客流係租金的函數**。租金低，你捱得住 13 蚊一碗；租金一高，同一碗粉要賣 20 蚊才活，而賣 20 蚊就冇咗附近嗰班固定客。

**投不投：觀察。** 公開資料只給了單碗價同日單量，冇租金、冇淨利、冇幾個人幹活、亦唔知有冇加價。算唔出回本周期，就唔應該話「可投」。要投這一類，我要看三個數：月租佔營業額比例、一碗粉嘅實際毛利、以及流量退潮後第 4 週嘅日單量。

至於流量本身：300 萬曝光帶來嘅係一次性脈衝，房租同人工係按月照付。所以我從來唔會因為一條片去評估一間店，我只會問——**冇咗條片，佢仲賣唔賣得 100 碗？**

再講白啲：呢類店值得學嘅唔係「等爆紅」，而係「守住低租金」。同樣一碗 13 蚊嘅粉，開喺租金貴三倍嘅商場，一日賣 200 碗都要蝕。所以真正嘅護城河係位置同租約，唔係味道，亦唔係流量。味道決定你捱唔捱得住，租金決定你捱得幾久。

如果你要我從呢件事抽一條可執行嘅判斷：**做小店，先算租金佔營業額幾成，再談出品。**超過三成，你就要靠爆紅續命；低過兩成，你就有本錢等。呢條線比任何流量技巧都硬。

\[Zheng Ge's Take\] Column: Zheng Ge's blog commentary | Date: 2026-11-19

One short video took a noodle shop tucked beside a vocational college from 20 bowls a day to 200. My first reaction was not 'what a story' but 'at thirteen yuan a bowl, how does this place survive at all'.

1. First the facts: from 20 bowls to 200

In Nanshan, Shenzhen, a noodle shop sits slightly off the beaten track near Shenzhen Polytechnic, run by Uncle Qin and his family. On 12 November a blogger working nearby filmed the shop downstairs from the office; the clip then climbed the local trending lists on Weibo and Douyin and drew more than three million views.

Daily sales went from 20 bowls to 200. Office workers and students travelled over to queue, including students from the college itself. Beef noodles cost thirteen yuan a bowl and nothing else goes above twenty; the clip has been seen more than three million times.

Source: 鳳凰網 | https://feng.ifeng.com/c/8y6DgBicF86

2. Where the money comes from at thirteen yuan a bowl

Thirteen yuan a bowl, minus ingredients, utilities and wages, leaves a thin margin. Shops like this survive on three stacked conditions: a location obscure enough for cheap rent; output fast enough that one stove serves many bowls a day; and a fixed customer base of nearby students and workers rather than tourists.

So when the traffic arrived, the natural response was to fill capacity, not to raise prices. Raising them would push away the regulars who carry the shop through the quiet months.

Source: 鳳凰網 | https://feng.ifeng.com/c/8y6DgBicF86

3. Why this kind of shop is the one that goes viral

There must be tens of thousands of shops like this in Shenzhen, so why this one? I put it down to contrast: out-of-the-way, shabby frontage, low prices, an honest older couple - all four together is exactly the 'authenticity' the platforms want. The algorithm does not push the best food; it pushes the place that looks most like the one near you.

The second condition is verifiability. Thirteen yuan a bowl, two hundred bowls a day: anyone understands those two numbers without expertise, which makes the story repeatable, shareable and locally recommendable. A restaurant at three hundred yuan a head could taste ten times better and still not travel the same way, because most people cannot see themselves in it.

Which leads to an uncomfortable conclusion: going viral is a content event, not a business event. A content event delivers one pulse; a business event is monthly rent, wages and consistent output. The two get conflated all the time, which is why every viral shop is followed by a wave of copycats who take on a lease and put it up for transfer six months later.

Source: 香港商報 | https://www.hkcd.com/

3. My take: is this a business worth copying

What is valuable here is not the sudden fame but the survival line it exposes: footfall is a function of rent. With low rent you can sell at thirteen yuan; once rent rises the same bowl must sell at twenty to survive, and at twenty the regulars disappear.

Invest or not: watch. The public account gives only the per-bowl price and daily volume - no rent, no net profit, no headcount, no word on whether prices moved. Without a payback period I will not call it investable. To judge this category I want three numbers: rent as a share of turnover, actual gross margin per bowl, and daily volume in week four after the traffic fades.

As for the traffic itself: three million views is a one-off pulse, while rent and wages are paid monthly. I never value a shop on a viral clip; I ask whether it still sells a hundred bowls without the clip.
