1943年的悲痛：被遺忘的潮汕大饑荒The Pain of 1943: The Forgotten Chaoshan Famine

2026年9月28日 23:14:21Sep 28, 2026 23:14:21 歷史記憶Historical Memory 潮汕Chaoshan
【鄭哥點評】【ZHENG GE REVIEW】

今日上網，見到呢個話題——「1943年的悲痛：潮汕大饑荒：那些被遺忘的歷史」。

作為汕尾人，我阿爺阿嫲都係經歷過果個年代。我從細就聽老一輩講過「響馬年間」或者「一粒米貴過黃金」嘅故事，但係從來冇深入去了解過。

今日我認真查咗一下，發覺呢個唔係故事，而係我哋先輩集體嘅傷痛。

歷史背景：1943年，潮汕地區究竟發生咗乜嘢？

1943年，潮汕地區——即係而家嘅汕頭、潮州、揭陽、汕尾——爆發咗一場極為嚴重嘅大饑荒。

傷亡人數：歷史研究指出，呢次饑荒造成嘅死亡人數，保守估計數十萬人，亦有研究認為超過一百萬人。具體數字至今仍有爭議，但係大量家庭破碎，村莊十室九空，係不爭嘅事實。

死亡原因：

  • 旱災：1942年至1943年，潮汕地區持續大旱，溪流斷流，農田龜裂，晚造失收
  • 蝗災：旱災之後，蝗蟲鋪天蓋地而來，將僅存嘅農作物吞噬殆盡
  • 日軍侵略：當時潮汕已被日軍佔領，交通封鎖、物資流通受阻，本地存糧被日軍徵用
  • 通貨膨脹：物價飛漲，一擔米嘅價格可以在一個月內暴漲十倍甚至數十倍

我阿爺點樣捱過果年

我阿爺曾經講過——

「響馬年，捱到樹皮都食埋。有啲人去山上掘觀音土（即係白泥）整餅食，消化唔到，大便都痾唔出，好多人係咁样死嘅。」

觀音土，白泥，高嶺土。食落去感覺饱咗，但其實唔會消化，長期食用會導致腸道堵塞。

我阿爺話，果年最容易死嘅係老人家同小朋友——老人家身體捱唔住，小朋友脾胃弱，觀音土一食就出事。

潮汕人點解要離鄉別井

呢次饑荒之後，大量潮汕人選擇離鄉。

一係走佬去香港、澳門、南洋（東南亞）；一係走大陸內地，去貴州、雲南、四川。

我哋而家喺香港、長洲、薄扶林見到嘅潮汕人社群，好多都係果個年代走難出去嘅後代。

呢個就係點解潮汕人社群喺香港、海外特別大——唔係因為選擇，而係因為生存。

被遺忘的傷痛

呢個歷史，我從細只係聽過零星故事，但係從未見過正式嘅歷史記載。

去過汕頭、潮州旅遊，你會見到騎樓、文化遺址、小食——但係你唔會見到任何關於1943年饑荒嘅紀念碑或者博物館。

呢個唔係因為人們想忘記，而係因為呢個題目太過沉重，亦太過敏感。

戰爭、死亡、饑荒——呢啲話題唔係旅遊推广會提到嘅嘢。

但係對於我哋後代，如果唔知道先輩捱過乜嘢，就唔會明白點解老一輩成日話「食飯唔好剩」——因為佢哋真係見過餓死嘅人。

寫喺最後

我今日寫呢篇，唔係為咗揭傷疤，而係為咗記住。

記住先輩捱過嘅苦，珍惜而家可以食飽飯嘅日子。

一粒米，來得不容易。

Today online, I came across this topic — "The Pain of 1943: The Forgotten Chaoshan Famine — Those Forgotten Histories."

As someone from Shanwei, my grandparents both lived through that era. Since I was small, I heard the elders talk about "the bandit years" or "when a single grain of rice cost more than gold" — but I never looked into it deeply.

Today I researched seriously, and discovered: this is not a story. It is our ancestors' collective trauma.

Historical Background: What Actually Happened in the Chaoshan Region in 1943?

In 1943, the Chaoshan region — now Shantou, Chaozhou, Jieyang, and Shanwei — experienced an extremely severe famine.

Casualties: Historical research estimates the death toll at conservatively tens of thousands, with some studies suggesting over one million. The exact figures remain disputed, but shattered families and villages emptied of nine out of ten households are undeniable facts.

Causes of Death:

  • Drought: 1942–1943, persistent severe drought in Chaoshan, streams ran dry, farmland cracked, late crops failed completely
  • Locust plague: After the drought, locusts descended in swarms, devouring whatever crops remained
  • Japanese invasion: Chaoshan was occupied by Japanese forces; transportation was blocked, supplies couldn't circulate, local food stocks were seized by the Japanese military
  • Inflation: Prices skyrocketed; a single担 of rice could surge tenfold or even dozens of times within one month

How My Grandfather Survived That Year

My grandfather once said —

"The bandit year, we ate everything including tree bark. Some people dug up觀音土 (white clay) from the mountains to make clay cakes to eat. It couldn't be digested — people couldn't even poop. Many died this way."

觀音土, white clay, kaolin. Eating it made you feel full, but it couldn't be digested. Long-term consumption caused intestinal blockage.

My grandfather said: the easiest to die that year were the elderly and children — elderly bodies couldn't take it, children's stomachs were weak, and one dose of clay would do them in.

Why the Chaoshan People Left Their Homeland

After this famine, large numbers of Chaoshan people chose to leave.

Some fled to Hong Kong, Macau, Nanyang (Southeast Asia). Others went inland to Guizhou, Yunnan, Sichuan.

The Chaoshan community we see today in Hong Kong, on Cheung Chau, in Pokfulam — many are descendants of those who fled during that era.

This is why the Chaoshan community is so prominent in Hong Kong and overseas — not by choice, but by survival.

The Forgotten Pain

This history — I only heard fragments as a child, never saw formal historical records.

Visit Shantou or Chaozhou for tourism, and you'll see arcade houses, cultural heritage sites, local snacks — but you won't find any memorial or museum dedicated to the 1943 famine.

This isn't because people want to forget. It's because the topic is too heavy, and too sensitive.

War, death, famine — these aren't topics tourism promotion will mention.

But for us descendants: if we don't know what our ancestors endured, we won't understand why the elderly always say "don't leave food in your bowl" — because they truly saw people starve to death.

Written at the End

I wrote this today not to reopen wounds, but to remember.

Remember what our ancestors endured, and cherish the days we can eat our fill.

A single grain of rice does not come easily.
