《一個eSIM激活，部手機就變成AI助手——電信運營商想靠呢樣嘢擺脫管道宿命》

2026年10月4日 14:00 AI科技
【鄭哥點評】欄目：鄭哥博客點評｜日期：2026-10-04｜關鍵詞：AI-eSIM、Token經濟、電信運營商、算力、中國移動、鄭哥Hany

引子：部手機以前係通訊工具，而家變成咗乜嘢？

我係1998年開始做外貿，當時最緊要嘅嘢係邊部傳真機快啲、邊個仔幾時到深圳。

今日，我身邊嘅年輕人問我：「鄭哥，你部手機係AI終端未？」

我望一望我部華為Mate，嘆咗口氣：「我仲喺度學習緊。」

呢個世代變化嘅速度，比我哋當年做外貿集裝箱周期仲要快。

第一幕：一個eSIM升級為AI入口——中國移動點樣佈局

2026年5月8日，中国移动喺上海搞咗個「2026移動雲大會」。

我睇新聞，見到一個好有意思嘅產品發布——AI-eSIM。

中國移動介紹：每一台終端設備，通過芯片層創新，可以獲得一個可信嘅數字身份，然後內置模型能力，eSIM一鍵升級為AI入口。

副總經理陳懷達嘅說法好直接：

「AI-eSIM可以應用於智能玩具、智能家電、智能眼鏡、無人機、機械人等眾多場景，實現AI終端『開機即聯網、聯網即智能』。」

翻譯成我哋做生意嘅語言：以前一部手機係為人類服務，而家一部手機自己就係一個智能體（AI Agent）。

你對住部手機講：「幫我下單10萬件聖誕玩具。」

部手機自己就做晒——揾工廠、分析價格、生成合同、安排出貨。

呢個就係Token經濟最前端嘅入口。

第二幕：電信運營商從「賣位元組」到「賣Token」——本質上係同一樣嘢？

做貿易嘅人都知：邊個掌握分銷渠道，邊個就係王。

語音時代，電信運營商賣分鐘——你打電話，按分鐘俾錢。

流量時代，電信運營商賣GB——你上網，按流量俾錢。

Token時代，電信運營商賣乜？

賣AI推理同生成嘅智能能力。

客戶唔再單純為傳輸帶寬、GPU佔用時長俾錢，而係為AI輸出嘅結果俾錢。

呢個係一次計價單位嘅根本性轉變。

但係——我做生意26年，我知呢個轉型有幾難。

電信運營商嘅優勢： - 網絡確定性：模型部署喺運營商自有數據中心，用戶通過運營商網絡訪問，可以喺網絡層面進行端到端優化——低時延、高穩定性 - 18億個人用戶+7300萬政企客戶嘅銷售觸達網絡——我哋做貿易嘅知道，搭建分銷渠道係最貴嘅嘢，佢哋已經有晒 - 幾十年套餐運營經驗——分級定價、預付費模式，佢哋玩咗幾十年

問題係： - 組織架構係為電信網絡設計，唔係為AI應用生態設計 - 人才結構係電信工程師為主，唔係AI應用開發者為主 - 文化係電信公司文化，唔係互聯網公司文化

「管道化」嘅命運，唔係靠推出幾個套餐就可以擺脫。

第三幕：AI-eSIM係真正嘅突破——芯片層創新先至係核心

我仔細研究咗下AI-eSIM嘅技術描述，發現佢哋強調「芯片層創新」。

呢個細節好重要。

以前嘅eSIM，只係一個細小小嘅芯片，存儲運營商嘅網絡認證信息。

今日嘅AI-eSIM，芯片本身就內置模型能力——唔再需要走雲端，部終端自己就係一個智能體。

呢個改變，顛覆咗三樣嘢：

第一：時延消失。

AI推理全部喺本地完成，唔需要走雲端往返，語音命令、機械臂控制、無人機導航——全部即時反應。

第二：私隱保障。

醫療數據、工廠工藝參數、財務信息——唔使上雲，直接喺設備本地處理，數據唔離開工廠。

第三：離線智能。

偏遠礦區、海上油田、野外作業——以前冇網絡就冇AI，而家只要有電，設備本身就係AI專家。

呢三樣顛覆，係雲端AI永遠做唔到嘅。

第四幕：運營商嘅真正考驗——生態封閉程度決定價值

我哋做外貿嘅人，最擅長嘅嘢就係判斷：

一個平台，係封閉生態定係開放生態？

越封閉——越值錢。

越開放——越快被淘汰。

電信運營商嘅AI-eSIM生態，取決於兩樣嘢：

第一：設備綁定程度。

如果AI-eSIM只喺中國移動網絡能用——佢就係封閉生態，值錢。

如果AI-eSIM跨運營商通用——佢就係開放生態，慢慢變成另一個「流量包」。

第二：應用生態控制力。

如果運營商能夠建立自己嘅應用商店、AI服務平台——佢就係蘋果模式，值錢。

如果運營商只係提供基礎管道，应用都係第三方——佢就係安卓模式，利潤被壓縮。

我嘅判斷：AI-eSIM係一次有意義嘅嘗試，但係運營商嘅基因決定咗佢哋難以成為AI時代嘅蘋果。

蘋果公司之所以成功，唔只係因為佢有芯片，而係因為佢有整個生態系統嘅控制權——呢個係幾十年嘅互聯網思維累積出嚟嘅，唔係靠幾個5G基站就能做到。

鄭哥三句話

第一句：AI-eSIM係有意義嘅技術創新，但唔係運營商嘅救命稻草。 芯片層創新令到終端設備可以本地運行AI，呢個方向係啱嘅。但係從「技術創新」到「商業成功」，中間仲有十万八千里——生態建設、用戶習慣、行業標準，每一關都係大考。

第二句：Token經濟嘅真正價值，唔喺Token本身，而喺生態封閉程度。 當每一個運營商都想做蘋果，但冇一個運營商有蘋果嘅生態控制力，結果就係個個都賣緊同質化嘅基礎服務，價格內卷，利潤攤薄。我哋做貿易嘅，見呢種場面見得多晒。

第三句：我哋可以點樣利用呢個趨勢？ 如果你係製造業老闆，關注AI-eSIM生態入面嘅工業物聯網設備——呢個可能係工廠智能化嘅下一個風口。如果你係軟件開發者，考慮為AI-eSIM設備開發本地AI應用——呢個係一個新嘅賽道。但係無論你做乜，記住：基礎設施供應商係辛苦錢，生態建設者先至係大錢。

一句話總結

AI-eSIM令每一台終端設備都成為AI入口，電信運營商終於搵到一個擺脫「管道化」嘅技術抓手。但係技術創新唔等於商業成功——運營商有網絡優勢、渠道優勢，缺嘅係互聯網生態基因。Token經濟嘅價值，取決於生態封閉程度；封閉生態係蘋果模式，開放生態係流量包模式。選擇邊個运营商合作，先問：你綁得幾實？

Intro: A phone used to be a communication tool — what has it turned into now?

I started in export in 1998. Back then the things that mattered most were which fax machine was faster and when the guy would get to Shenzhen.

Today the young people around me ask: "Hany, is your phone an AI device yet?"

I look at my Huawei Mate, sigh, and say: "I'm still learning."

Generations are turning over faster than the container cycles we worked to in the old export days.

Act 1: One eSIM upgraded into an AI gateway — how China Mobile is laying it out

On May 8, 2026, China Mobile held its "2026 Mobile Cloud Conference" in Shanghai.

Reading the news, I came across a product launch that interested me — AI-eSIM.

China Mobile explains it this way: through chip-level innovation, every terminal device can get a trusted digital identity, and then built-in model capability, so that one tap upgrades the eSIM into an AI gateway.

Deputy General Manager Chen Huaida put it plainly:

"AI-eSIM can be used in smart toys, smart home appliances, smart glasses, drones, robots and many other scenarios, so that AI devices are 'online the moment they power on, and intelligent the moment they are online.'"

In the language of our trade: a phone used to serve a human being; now a phone is itself an agent (AI Agent).

You say to the phone: "Order 100,000 Christmas toys for me."

The phone does the whole thing itself — finds factories, analyses prices, generates the contract, arranges shipment.

This is the front door of the token economy.

Act 2: Telecom operators going from "selling bytes" to "selling tokens" — is it essentially the same thing?

Anyone in trade knows: whoever controls the distribution channel is king.

In the voice era, operators sold minutes — you made a call and paid by the minute.

In the data era, operators sold GB — you went online and paid by the gigabyte.

In the token era, what do operators sell?

They sell the intelligence of AI inference and generation.

Customers no longer pay simply for bandwidth or for GPU time; they pay for what the AI produces.

This is a fundamental change in the unit of billing.

But — 26 years in business — I know how hard this kind of shift is.

The operators' advantages: - Network determinism: the model is deployed in the operator's own data centre and users reach it over the operator's network, so optimisation can happen end to end at the network layer — low latency, high stability - 1.8 billion consumer users plus 73 million government and enterprise customers — a sales reach network. Those of us in trade know that building a distribution channel is the most expensive part; they already have it - Decades of experience running tariff plans — tiered pricing, prepaid models; they have been playing that game for decades

The problems: - The org structure was designed for a telecom network, not for an AI application ecosystem - The talent base is mostly telecom engineers, not AI application developers - The culture is a telecom company culture, not an internet company culture

The fate of becoming a "dumb pipe" cannot be escaped just by launching a few more tariff plans.

Act 3: AI-eSIM is the real breakthrough — chip-level innovation is the core

I went carefully through the technical description of AI-eSIM, and saw that they stress "chip-level innovation".

That detail matters a great deal.

The old eSIM was just a very small chip holding the operator's network credentials.

Today's AI-eSIM has model capability built into the chip itself — no trip to the cloud needed; the device is an agent in its own right.

That change overturns three things:

First: latency disappears.

All AI inference happens locally, with no round trip to the cloud — voice commands, robot-arm control, drone navigation, all responding instantly.

Second: privacy.

Medical data, factory process parameters, financial information — none of it has to go to the cloud; it is processed locally on the device, and the data never leaves the factory.

Third: offline intelligence.

Remote mining areas, offshore oil fields, field work — in the past, no network meant no AI; now, as long as there is power, the device itself is the AI expert.

Those three overthrows are things cloud AI can never pull off.

Act 4: The operators' real test — how closed the ecosystem is decides the value

People in export are best at one thing: judging.

Is a platform a closed ecosystem or an open one?

The more closed — the more valuable.

The more open — the faster it gets wiped out.

The operators' AI-eSIM ecosystem comes down to two things:

First: how tightly devices are bound to it.

If AI-eSIM only works on China Mobile's network, it is a closed ecosystem, and it is worth money.

If AI-eSIM works across operators, it is an open ecosystem, and it slowly turns into just another "data bundle".

Second: control over the application ecosystem.

If the operator can build its own app store and AI service platform, it is the Apple model, and it is worth money.

If the operator only provides the basic pipe and the applications are all third-party, it is the Android model, and margins get squeezed.

My call: AI-eSIM is a meaningful attempt, but the operators' DNA means they will find it hard to become the Apple of the AI era.

Apple succeeded not only because it has chips, but because it has control of an entire ecosystem — that came from decades of accumulated internet thinking, and a few 5G base stations will not get you there.

Hany's Three Sentences

First: AI-eSIM is a meaningful piece of technical innovation, but it is not the operators' lifeline. Chip-level innovation lets a terminal run AI locally, and that direction is right. But from "technical innovation" to "commercial success" is an enormous distance — ecosystem building, user habits, industry standards; every gate is a major exam.

Second: the real value of the token economy is not in the token itself, but in how closed the ecosystem is. When every operator wants to be Apple but not one of them has Apple's grip on an ecosystem, the result is that they all sell the same commoditised basic service, prices grind against each other, and margins get thinner. Those of us in trade have seen that film plenty of times.

Third: how can we make use of this trend? If you are a manufacturer, watch the industrial IoT devices inside the AI-eSIM ecosystem — that may be the next opening in factory intelligence. If you are a software developer, consider building local AI applications for AI-eSIM devices — that is a new lane. But whatever you do, remember: infrastructure suppliers earn hard money; ecosystem builders earn the big money.

In one sentence

AI-eSIM turns every terminal device into an AI gateway, and telecom operators have finally found a technical handle to escape the "dumb pipe" fate. But technical innovation is not commercial success — the operators have network advantages and channel advantages; what they lack is internet ecosystem DNA. The value of the token economy depends on how closed the ecosystem is: a closed ecosystem is the Apple model, an open one is the data-bundle model. Before you choose an operator to work with, ask first: how tightly can you be tied in?
