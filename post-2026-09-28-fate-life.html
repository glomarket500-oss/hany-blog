人到老年越来越信命？Do We Believe in Fate as We Age?

2026年9月28日 23:26:07Sep 28, 2026 23:26:07 人生感悟Life 老鄉故事Story
【鄭哥點評】【ZHENG GE REVIEW】

我老鄉阿強，喺我哋汕尾老家，唔係最有錢，但係最為人津津樂道。

佢69年出世，初中畢業就走出去打工，捱咗十幾年，捱到30幾歲先至開始搞電子商務。

阿強一開始做淘寶，乜都賣——服裝、數碼配件、玩具，試過爆單，亦試過压貨压到周身債。有一年春節前，破產嘅消息傳到老家，老母親喺屋企喊咗成晚。

然後坐咗6年。

坐期間，老父走咗，佢都冇辦法回來送終。

放出嚟嗰年，佢35歲。

第二幕：出嚟之後半年——全村議論紛紛

放出嚟之後，佢返咗老家。

阿強做咗一件事，轟動成條村——

佢娶咗個25歲嘅女仔，喺銀行做嘢嘅，漂亮，城里買咗樓，開住一台二十幾萬嘅車。女方冇收禮金、冇收三金，婚禮50幾圍，全部係女方出錢。

成棚嘢加埋，至少一百幾十萬。

全村人議論：阿強何德何能？一個坐過監嘅人，一貧如洗，憑乜嘢？

第三幕：阿強靠乜嘢——我哋老家人人都唔知

我哋老家人人都有自己嘅版本。

有人話：「阿強係律师，認識人多，出嚟之後靠關係搵到好多女仔。」

有人話：「阿強識靠女人，專業食軟飯。」

有人話：「阿強中咗彩票。」

阿強自己從來冇解釋。

我問過佢：「你究竟點解可以咁快翻站起身？」

佢笑笑：「鄭哥，命裡有時終須有。」

第四幕：人到老年——越來越信命

阿強今年50幾歲，事業穩定，仔女雙全。

佢而家成日掛喺嘴邊嘅一句說話：「人到中年，你不得不信命。」

我理解阿強講呢句說話嘅意思——

佢並唔係話我哋要坐以待斃，靜靜地等待命運降臨。

而係話：當你發現身邊嘅人，無論幾勤力、幾聰明，結局都差天共地；當你發現有啲人，無論幾襟聰明、幾襟努力，結果都係负债累累；而有啲人，貌似糊糊，但係關鍵時刻總係遇到貴人。

你會開始相信：呢個世界，有一隻手，唔係完全喺你控制範圍之內。

第五幕：命運同實力——邊個重要？

我唔係完全同意阿強，但係我理解佢。

我認識一個潮汕老闆，佢講過一句說話，我記到而家：

「我今日嘅成就，三分靠實力，七分靠運氣。但係我從來唔會對外宣稱三分靠實力，因為咁樣會顯得我好蠢。」

電子商務呢20年，我見過太多人——

有人踩中風口，豬都會飛；有人勤力到死，但係踩中平台收緊，一舂回到解放前。

有啲人，能力平平，但係老老實實做生意，一步一步行，結果穩穩當當。

有啲人，能力好強，但係貪心過度，加杠杆，最後一舂就執笠。

鄭哥三句話

第一句：命運係一部分，但係唔係全部。 阿強嘅故事唔係話你坐喺度等運到，而係話：當你盡曬全力，結果仍然唔理想，唔好太責怪自己。呢個世界有隨機性，有運氣，有我哋控制唔到嘅變量。接受呢個事實，但係唔放棄努力。

第二句：財富係暫存嘅，信用係長期嘅。 阿強放出嚟之後，可以半年內結婚，唔係靠運氣，而係靠佢之前積累嘅人脈同信用。出來之後，有人願意幫佢，有人願意嫁俾佢——呢個唔係命，而係佢過去做人累積落嚟嘅社會資本。

第三句：「信命」唔係消極，而係豁達。 老人家話「信命」，唔係叫你投降，而係話你學懂接受：有些事情我哋控制唔到，我哋能夠控制嘅，就係自己嘅努力同態度。盡人事，聽天命——呢個係老炮兒多年嚟最真實嘅體會。

一句話總結

阿強嘅故事告訴我哋：人到中年，信命唔係認輸，而係睇清世界運行的規則之後，選擇繼續前行。電子商務呢行，起起跌跌係常態，運氣係一部分，但係堅持、做人、累積信用——呢啲先係我哋能夠控制嘅野。

My fellow villager Ah-Keung, from our hometown in Shanwei, isn't the richest — but he's the most talked about.

Born in '69, he left home after junior high and worked odd jobs for over a decade. He didn't start e-commerce until his mid-30s.

He started on Taobao, selling everything — clothing, digital accessories, toys. He'd experienced explosive orders, and also inventory pressure that left him buried in debt. One year before Chinese New Year, news of his bankruptcy reached our hometown. His old mother cried all night at home.

Then he served six years.

During that time, his father passed away. Ah-Keung couldn't return to say goodbye.

When he got out, he was 35.

Act 2: Half a Year After Release — The Whole Village Gossiped

After his release, he went back to our hometown.

Ah-Keung did one thing that shocked the entire village —

He married a 25-year-old woman who worked at a bank. Beautiful, she'd already bought an apartment in the city and drove a car worth over two hundred thousand. No bride price, no gold jewelry, no nothing from her side. Their wedding had over fifty tables, all paid for by the bride's family.

Add it all up — at least a million or more in value.

The whole village talked: What does Ah-Keung have going for him? A man who'd been to prison, penniless — on what grounds?

Act 3: What Did Ah-Keung Rely On — No One in Our Village Knew

Everyone in our village had their own version.

Some said: "Ah-Keung knows lawyers, knows lots of people. After getting out, he used connections to meet lots of women."

Some said: "Ah-Keung knows how to leech off women. A professional gigolo."

Some said: "Ah-Keung won the lottery."

Ah-Keung never explained himself.

I asked him: "How did you manage to get back on your feet so fast?"

He smiled and said: "Zheng Ge, what's fated will come eventually."

Act 4: As We Age — We Gradually Believe in Fate

Ah-Keung is now in his mid-50s, stable career, kids on both sides — meaning a son and a daughter.

What he says all the time now: "When you hit middle age, you can't help but believe in fate."

I understand what Ah-Keung means by this —

He's not saying we should sit and wait for fate to arrive.

He's saying: when you see people around you, no matter how hard-working, how smart, their endings are worlds apart; when you see people who are incredibly clever, incredibly diligent, but still end up buried in debt; while some people, seemingly muddling through, always seem to meet贵 人 at critical moments.

You start to believe: there's a hand in this world, not entirely within your control.

Act 5: Fate vs. Effort — Which Matters More?

I don't fully agree with Ah-Keung, but I understand him.

I know a Chaoshan businessman who said something I've remembered ever since:

"My achievements today are 30% effort, 70% luck. But I would never tell anyone outside that my success is only 30% effort — because that would make me look stupid."

Over 20 years in e-commerce, I've seen too many people —

Some caught the wind and even pigs could fly; some worked themselves to death but stepped on a platform crackdown and one shot brought them back to square one.

Some with average abilities, but honest in business, moving step by step, ended up stable and secure.

Some with great abilities, but overly greedy, leveraged up, and one day just collapsed.

Zheng Ge's Three Points

Point 1: Fate is part of it, but not all of it. Ah-Keung's story isn't saying you should sit and wait for luck. It's saying: when you've given your all and the result still isn't ideal, don't blame yourself too much. There's randomness in this world, luck, variables beyond our control. Accept this fact, but don't give up on effort.

Point 2: Wealth is temporary, credit is long-term. After Ah-Keung got out, he could get married within half a year — not because of luck, but because of the connections and credit he'd accumulated before. After getting out, someone was willing to help him, someone was willing to marry him — that's not fate, that's the social capital he built up through how he treated people.

Point 3: "Believing in fate" isn't passive — it's enlightened. When elderly people say "believe in fate," they're not telling you to surrender. They're saying: learn to accept that some things are beyond our control. What we can control is our own effort and attitude. Do your best, then listen to heaven — this is the truest reflection from an old trader's many years.

One-Line Summary

Ah-Keung's story tells us: in middle age, believing in fate isn't admitting defeat — it's choosing to keep going after seeing clearly how the world operates. In e-commerce, ups and downs are normal. Luck is part of it. But persistence, character, building credit — these are what we can control.
